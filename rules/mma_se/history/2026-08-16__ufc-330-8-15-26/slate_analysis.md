# UFC — Makhachev vs. Machado Garry (8/15/26) — Slate Strategy

## The short version

1. This is a 12-fight UFC title card where the three most expensive fighters are all dominant grapplers.
2. DraftKings MMA pays for takedowns, control time, and strikes, so a wrestler who controls a long fight scores the most points.
3. The crowd will load up on Islam Makhachev, Mackenzie Dern, and the cheap favorite Charles Johnson.
4. The most obvious build — those three plus Gillian Robertson — will be copied by hundreds of teams, and a copied lineup splits its prize even when it hits.
5. The decision that separates entries this week is which ONE of the three expensive grapplers you leave out, and who takes that money instead.
6. A sharp keeps six fighters who can each win their fight, and gets different by using an under-trusted favorite, not a hopeless cheap fighter.
7. The biggest trap is a price: Charles Johnson is picked by about 4 of every 10 teams, far more than his projected points deserve.
8. Your five entries must not all lean on the same coin-flip fights, or one bad result kills them all at once.

## Build it like a sharp

A numbered walk-through of the decisions a sharp makes on this slate. Suggestions here are in sharp voice; every pick is yours.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors from the $9.0–9.6K grappler trio but takes one or two, not three — Makhachev (50%), Dern (44%), Orolbai (40%) are anchor-equivalence twins, and which one he LEAVES OUT is the differentiation. Ribovics (29%) is the fourth anchor the crowd underprices.
2. **The leverage piece.** It comes from under-trusted converters, not automatic darts: Abdul-Malik (16%), Njokuani (15%), Tobias (15%), Chapolin (17%), Magny (17%). moklovin carries a true low-picked piece in only ~1 of 3 lineups — the sub-10% dart (Stoltzfus, Barboza, Wells) is optional, taken only when its win case beats the converter it replaces.
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Carrying Makhachev + Dern means sharing that slice with ~523 of the 2,378 entries before a punch lands — and your measured MMA fields duplicate 1.7–6.6× MORE than that naive math; the most-copied lineup in your past SE ran 22 deep. He breaks at least one of the top pairs.
4. **The salary shape.** Two big anchors (say $9,200 + $9,000) leave $31,800 for four slots — $7,950 each — which is exactly why the whole field funnels into C. Johnson and Robertson. The real decision is which anchor to drop so a mid-tier converter fits instead of a third piece of copied chalk.
5. **Five entries, three contests.** The 891-field flat 3-Max rewards the tight six-winners chalk-anchored build; the two top-heavy SEs reward the differentiated one — and the SE bullet is where your last four slates leaked discipline, so it gets built FIRST, to the same rules. Across all five, no two entries share more than one coin-flip fighter (Turner/Fernandes, Gore/Luque, C. Johnson/Chapolin, Brahimaj/Magny) — shared coin flips die together.
6. **The pre-lock check.** Can all six answer "how does he WIN?" in one sentence? Is there at least one under-trusted converter? Did you avoid the full Makhachev + Dern + C. Johnson copy? Is any sub-10% piece there on merit, not obligation? Would you still like the entry if Makhachev scores 105, not 115?

Target envelope in one line: moklovin runs ~27.8% ownership per slot with a leverage piece in about a third of his lineups — six winners, one under-trusted converter, one broken chalk pair.
