# UFC Shanghai — Nurmagomedov vs. Song (8/29/26) — Slate Strategy

## The short version
1. This is a 13-fight UFC card in Shanghai, played in two small single-entry contests where only a near-first finish pays real money.
2. Points in MMA come from finishes, takedowns and strike volume — and this card is stacked with favorites priced to finish their fights early.
3. The crowd will pile onto the main-event wrestler Umar Nurmagomedov and the hometown kickboxer Liu Ce.
4. Your own history says one well-liked mid-priced fighter always ends up far more popular than projected, and Liu Ce is the obvious candidate this week.

7. The biggest trap is paying popularity prices for fighters whose most likely result is a low-scoring judges' decision — Sean Woodson and Jack Jenkins have exactly that shape this week.
8. The only truly unpopular players are the cheap underdogs Rojas and Borjas, and each needs a knockout to matter at all.

## Edges & tensions
This section lists the things that actually decide this slate, with the data behind each.
1. **Anchor-Equivalence:** Hasan ($9,600, 29% own) and Tsuruya ($9,700, 25%) project nearly identical (90.2 vs 91.6, DailyFan) — the field treats them as the same bet; Asakura ($9,400) carries similar win equity at only 21%.
2. **Umar's price dislocation:** the card's highest projection (99.4, DailyFan) costs LESS than Hasan and Tsuruya — the crowd's 45% is at least pointed at the right guy.
3. **Convergence watch:** on 15 of 15 measured cards, one mid-priced narrative name ran ~+15 points hotter than projected — Liu Ce ($8,700, 38% projected, hometown K-1 champion, fight −700 to end early) fits it exactly; plan as if he's ~50% owned.
4. **Duplication:** the field pairs Umar + Liu Ce in ~17.1% of lineups — about 122 of the 713 Clinch entries share that exact pairing (bundle chalk combos).
5. **Opponent shape (your 9 logged SE contests):** the field piles onto ~8 names at ~37.6% average ownership, and its losing half keeps buying 25%+-owned upper-mid ($8–10K) favorites who fail — that price shape, not any player, is the recurring trap.
6. **Trap-shaped prices TODAY:** Sean Woodson (+13 ownership ranks vs projection), Jack Jenkins (+12), Su Sumudaerji (+9), Denise Gomes (+8), Francesco Nuzzi (+8), Julia Polastri (+5) are all owned ahead of what their projections earn.
7. **Vendor vs crowd on the Yan fight:** DailyFan makes Yan Xiaonan the 57% favorite at just 16% ownership while the crowd takes Gomes at 31% — the favorite is half as popular as the dog.
8. **The only sub-10% pieces are binary:** Nilson Rojas ($6,600, 7%, ceiling 94.6) and Kevin Borjas ($6,500, 7%, ceiling 85.2) both need a knockout of a −760/−800 favorite; per the framework, the realistic differentiation tier on a card like this moves up into the 15–25% converters.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played
This section shows how the mass of entries will look, and how a pro's entry differs.
**The field:** the typical entry anchors Umar Nurmagomedov (45%) and adds Liu Ce (38% projected, likely ~50% real), then fills from the crowded dog tier — Gomes and Nelson at 31%, Jenkins at 28% — with Lawrence Lui (32%) the popular mid-anchor. Your logged fields put their top-3 chalk together in ~10.2% of lineups.
**A sharp:** the one named pro in your contests, moklovin, averages 29.8% ownership per slot and puts a sub-5% piece in about 1 of 4 lineups. Applied here: he can hold Umar, but he breaks the Umar+Liu pairing, sources his other slots from the 16–25% band (Asakura, Lima, Yan, Rodrigues), and every lineup is unique.
**The gap:** the field doubles up its two chalkiest names and buys decision-shaped dogs at 28–31%; the sharp keeps one chalk anchor and takes the same win equity at half the ownership one fight over.

## Build it like a sharp
The sharp's decisions in order, applied to this card. Suggestions here are the sharp archetype's, not commands.
1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors from the $9,400–$9,700 tier and pays for a FINISH path, not just a win. Candidates: Umar (45% — highest ceiling, cheapest of the top three), or one of the equivalence trio Hasan (29%) / Tsuruya (25%) / Asakura (21%). The framework's salary rule: 1–2 fighters at $9,000+, never more.
2. **The leverage piece.** A sharp's differentiation on this card lives in the 16–22% converters — Yan, Santiago, Xiong, Long, Perez — not in the 7% darts; Rojas and Borjas are knockout-or-zero, the exact profile your codified small-field rule benches. moklovin carries a true sub-5% piece in only ~1 of 4 lineups.
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Carrying Umar AND Liu Ce means sharing that spine with ~122 of 713 Clinch opponents before a punch lands. He also won't put the top-3 chalk together — your logged fields do that ~10% of the time.
4. **The salary shape.** Umar at $9,500 leaves $8,100 average for five slots; adding a second $9K+ name drops the rest to ~$7,700 average — which forces the build into the $7,500–8,999 band, exactly where the framework says the win probability per dollar is best. The real decision when it doesn't fit: which of Hasan/Tsuruya/Liu you drop, not which punt you add.
5. **Two contests, two different entries.** The 713 Clinch and 392 Flying Knee get different lineups (one lineup, one contest), differing on at least one conviction anchor — for example one entry on Umar, the other on the Hasan/Tsuruya/Asakura twin; or opposite sides of the Liu–Rodrigues fight. The 392 field can lean a notch chalkier; the 713 needs the extra separation.
6. **The pre-lock check.** Can you state in one sentence how EACH of the six wins his fight? Is there at most one fighter under $7,200? Did you avoid the Umar + Liu Ce pairing (or accept its ~122 co-owners knowingly)? Are you off both sides of any fight you took leverage in? Is the win-case ceiling sum over 600?

Target: the observed sharp envelope in YOUR contests — moklovin at ~29.8% ownership per slot, all-unique, a leverage piece in roughly 1 of 4 entries — matched on structure, beaten on selection.
