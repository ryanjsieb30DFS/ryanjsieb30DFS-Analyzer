# MMA Slate Strategy — UFC Fight Night Paris (Parnasse vs. Hooker), 9/5/26

## The short version

This section is the whole slate in eight plain sentences.

1. This is a 14-fight UFC card in Paris, and the crowd's money is concentrated on a handful of big favorites.
2. Points in MMA DFS come almost entirely from picking fighters who WIN their fights, and fights that end early (a knockout or submission) pay the most.
3. The articles call the middle of this price list ugly and hard to trust, so the crowd will pile even harder onto the few names it likes.
4. Nearly half the field will start their team with the main-event favorite, Salahdine Parnasse.
5. The one decision that most separates entries this week: the crowd is paying double the attention for some expensive finishers while Nathaniel Wood offers almost the same chance to win at a fraction of the attention.
6. A sharp (a consistently winning player) anchors on ONE popular favorite, not three, and finds separation in the mid-priced fighters the crowd undervalues.
7. The biggest trap is paying a high price for a fighter who can only score big with an early finish, in a fight the numbers say will be slow or go long.
8. Both of your contests allow one entry each, so each lineup is one shot — and the two lineups must not be the same bet made twice.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually matter on this card — tensions to build around, not commands.

1. **The Wood mispricing.** DailyFan gives Wood a 72% win chance at 16% ownership; Pinto gets 75% at 32% — the same wager at half the crowd.
2. **Anchor-equivalence.** Campbell ($9,300, 29% owned), Keita ($9,200, 26%), and Pinto ($9,100, 32%) are near-identical finish-needing anchors the field treats as interchangeable.
3. **The duplicated pair.** The field pairs Parnasse + Donchenko in ~17.1% of lineups — about 163 of the 951 Clinch entries share that start.
4. **The convergence name.** History says one mid-priced narrative favorite runs ~15 points over projected ownership; Duclos ($8,200, 36% projected, both articles' mid-tier #1, home crowd) is this card's candidate — plan as if he lands near 50%.
5. **Your opponents' habit.** In your 8 past comparable single-entry contests the same small field piles onto ~8 names at ~37.8% average ownership — expect that pile on Parnasse, Donchenko, Duclos, Dias, Pinto.
6. **The trap shape.** The prices the losing half keeps buying: 33 of 50 past traps were 25%+-owned, mostly in the $8-10K tier — popular players who fail, not long shots.
7. **Today's trap-shaped prices** (ownership ranked ahead of projection): Ziam, Ruziboev, Sola, Sy, Montenegro, Sygula. This is a screen, not a verdict — the ledger notes MMA winners often come FROM this list, so each fade below stands on structure, not this list.
8. **Vendor vs. Vegas.** DailyFan gives Sygula a 52% win chance; the betting line favors Cornolle at -125. Someone is wrong in a fight both are owned at just 16%.
9. **Article vs. vendor on Wood.** DailyFan projects Wood 4th on the whole slate (74.6); Brett doubts he pays off $9,200. That disagreement is exactly where the 16% ownership comes from.
10. **The shared coin-flip hazard.** Duclos and Dias are BOTH ~35% owned in a -110/-106 fight; with two single-entry lineups, putting the same side of any coin-flip fight in both makes them one bet (the set-diversity rule).

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section is the head-to-head read: what the mass of entries will look like, and what a winning player does differently.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry starts with Parnasse (45% owned) and adds one or two of Donchenko (38%), Duclos (36%), and Dias (35%), leaning on the $8-10K tier the way your past opponents always have (~8 names at ~37.8% average). The top three chalk names have landed together in 11.6% of your opponents' past lineups, and the most-copied lineup in those fields appeared 11 times.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The one observed pro in your contests (moklovin) runs about 29.8% ownership per slot, carries a low-owned piece in roughly 1 of 4 lineups, and anchors a favorite in about half his entries. Applied here: one anchor from the Parnasse/Campbell/Pinto tier — with a stated finish path — then separation from the 16%-owned tier (Wood, Lima, Cornolle) rather than a forced long shot.

**The gap.** The field pays 26-38% ownership for the finish-needing $9K names; a sharp gets nearly the same win equity at 16% through Wood, and refuses to hold the Parnasse + Donchenko pair that ~163 Clinch opponents will share.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks the decisions a sharp makes on this card, in order. Suggestions here are the sharp archetype's reasoning — the picks are yours, and construction happens in the Sim.

1. **The anchor decision.** Every $9,000+ slot needs a stated finish path, not just a win path (the salary rule: price buys win probability, not ceiling). The candidates with real paths: Parnasse (45%, -365 to finish), Campbell (29%, grappling domination that scores without a KO), Pinto (32%, fight -500 to end early). Campbell / Keita / Pinto are anchor-equivalence twins — a sharp holds one of the three, and notes Wood carries near-identical win equity at 16%.
2. **The leverage piece.** The observed pro carries a low-owned piece in roughly 1 of 4 lineups — so here it is optional, never forced. The live candidates by ownership: Wood 16%, Lima 16%, Cornolle 16%, then the true darts Hooker 8%, Peek 9%, Naimov 9% (each needs its finish to pay).
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Carrying Parnasse + Donchenko together means sharing that start with ~163 of the 951 Clinch opponents before a punch lands. If the pair gets split, the sharp keeps the lighter half (Donchenko, 38%) rather than the heavier (Parnasse, 45%) — splitting a pair only buys separation when you drop the more-shared name. Historically your opponents' top-3 chalk landed together in 11.6% of lineups; that block is one bet.
4. **The salary shape.** Parnasse's $9,500 leaves $8,100 average for five slots — workable. Parnasse + Campbell ($18,800) leaves $6,240 average, below almost the entire board, forcing two slots into the $6,700-7,000 dart tier. So the real decision is which ONE expensive name to drop, and the cheap slot must still be a decision floor (Charriere) or a live finisher (Andrusca, Soriano) — never salary filler with no win path.
5. **Two contests, two different entries.** The 951 Clinch pays top-heavy — the win is everything — so it gets the maximum-ceiling, duplication-safe build (no Parnasse+Donchenko pair, expect Duclos near 50% real ownership). The 588 Flying Knee can hold the steadier conviction build. The two entries must differ on at least one conviction anchor, and must not share the same side of a coin-flip fight (Duclos/Dias, Aljarouj/Sintes, Cornolle/Sygula) — a shared coin-flip resolves to zero in both entries at once.
6. **The pre-lock check.** Five yes/no questions against each built lineup:
   - Can you say in one sentence how each of the six WINS his fight?
   - Does every $9,000+ slot have a finish path, not just a win path?
   - Is the lineup free of the Parnasse + Donchenko pair?
   - Do the two entries differ on an anchor and avoid sharing any coin-flip side?
   - Is the cheap slot a decision floor or a live finisher — not math relief?

Close: the target envelope is moklovin's — about 29.8% ownership per slot, a low-owned piece in roughly 1 of 4 entries, and every entry a distinct answer to the slate. He has beaten you in 6 of 16 meetings by being right about winners, not by being contrarian.
