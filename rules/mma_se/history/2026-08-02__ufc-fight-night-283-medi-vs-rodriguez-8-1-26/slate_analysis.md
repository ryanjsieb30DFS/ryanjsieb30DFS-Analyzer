2. Points on this card come from fast knockouts — a fighter who wins early scores huge, and a fighter who wins slowly scores much less.

8. The biggest trap is putting the two most popular fighters, Medic and Cepo, on the same team — a big slice of the field will have that exact pair, so even a win gets shared with many people.
9. Your own recent pattern: the last two slates, the plan named the low-owned difference-makers and your entries played none of them — this week, actually rostering at least one is the point of the plan.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section describes the two ways this slate gets built, so you can build against one and toward the other.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry anchors Medic (42% projected ownership — and your logged history says the consensus favorite usually comes in even hotter than projected), adds one or two more of the Cepo/Oliveira/Rebecki hammer tier, then fills with the popular mid-priced names: Gugnon (34%), Todorovic (28%, the most-picked underdog), Nikolic (27%), Janicic (26%), Valentin (25%). The shared pairs above mean the mass of the field is holding nearly the same team with one or two spots changed.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The only pro tracked in your contests, moklovin (seen 5 times, beat you 2 of 5), runs about 30% ownership per roster spot — he rides chalk, not long shots — and carries a true sub-5% dart in only about a quarter of his lineups. Your last three slates of contest winners looked the same: six fighters who all won, roughly 31-40% average ownership, zero sub-10% darts, with the difference made by two or three MID-popularity favorites the field under-trusted. On this card, that translates to: anchor one or two hammers, then take the 13-16%-owned favorites (Rakic, Milosevic, Luciano) where the field takes the 30-42% versions of the same bet.

**The gap.** The field pays 30-42% popularity for early-knockout favorites; a sharp gets the same ~75% win chances at 13-16% popularity one tier over. That one substitution — a quiet favorite for a loud one — is where this slate's separation lives.

**How a sharp uses the key players:**

- **Medic ($9.4K, 42% DailyFan).** The field's #1 pick. A sharp holds him near or slightly under the field — he is a real "best first-round knockout odds on the slate" play (-375 to finish early) — but refuses to pair him with Cepo, because that exact pair sits on ~90+ teams. Anchoring Medic leaves $40.6K for five spots (about $8.1K each), which forces at least two sub-$7.5K fighters — his teammates are where the entry must get different.
- **Cepo ($9.1K, 36%).** Thirteen of his 14 wins came in round one, but the article calls him "legit bad" after two minutes: no cardio, no ground game, 0-2 in fights that reach round three. A sharp treats Medic/Cepo as one bet and carries one, not both — and remembers the live opposite side (Urbina, below).
- **Rebecki ($9.6K, 30%).** The slate's biggest favorite (-700) with a grappling path to the slate's top score (the article: "elite ceiling," averaging 107 DK per win). A sharp who anchors him accepts the salary squeeze — $40.4K left — and knows he is the popular four's safest win but not its safest ceiling (Prepolec is "sneaky," and Rebecki absorbs a lot of damage).
- **Oliveira ($9.2K, 30%).** Same ownership as Rebecki, same 110-point upside per the article, $400 cheaper. He is the substitutable twin — a sharp is happy holding whichever of the two the rest of the build affords, and won't hold both plus Medic (that's $28.2K on three spots).
- **Rakic ($9.3K, 13%).** The sharp's favorite kind of mistake by the field: a 75% win chance (DailyFan) at 1-in-8 popularity because he's "tough to click" after four straight losses. The article: there are real outcomes where he knocks Tybura out in two minutes. He is boom-or-bust — the bust is real — but at this popularity the boom is nearly unshared.
- **Milosevic ($9.5K, 16%).** An 80% win chance at 16% ownership — the second-biggest quiet favorite. The article gives her a path to 90-100 points through ground domination. Expensive, so anchoring her costs like a hammer but shares like a sleeper.
- **Gugnon ($8K, 34%) / Janicic ($8.2K, 26%).** The article likes BOTH sides — "both sides historically smash in wins" — and expects near-split action. The field leans Gugnon; the same bet at lower popularity is Janicic. A sharp picks a side rather than paying the 34% price for the coin-flip's popular half, and never carries both (opposite corners of one fight = one always scores near zero).
- **Todorovic ($7.7K, 28%).** The field's favorite underdog — 28% ownership on a 42% win chance, while his opponent Valentin has a 58% chance at 25%. A sharp notices the field is paying favorite-level popularity for the dog side of a coin flip and prefers the winning side of that math.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions in the order a sharp makes them. You pick every player; this is how the sharp archetype would hold them.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors this slate in the $9.1K-$9.6K hammer tier — but takes ONE, maybe two, never three. The candidates: Medic (42%), Rebecki (30%), Oliveira (30%), Cepo (36%). Two pairs inside that tier are near-identical bets: Oliveira/Rebecki (same 30% popularity, same 110 upside, $400 apart) and Medic/Cepo (same round-1-or-nothing profile). The cheaper or less-popular half of each pair is the same ticket at a discount — and remember Medic's quirk: five straight knockout wins under 105 points.
2. **The leverage piece.** A sharp's difference-maker this week is a quiet FAVORITE, not a long shot: Rakic (13%), Milosevic (16%), Luciano (13%), or the dog with a real plan, Urbina (15%). The observed pro envelope in your contests (moklovin: ~30% ownership per spot, a true dart in only ~24% of lineups) says one or two of these mid-popularity pivots per entry is the sharp dose — and your last two slates rostered zero of the named candidates, which is the leak to close. The Prepolec dart (7%) is optional seasoning, only if it beats the mid-own converter it would replace.
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Both halves of the crowd's pairs: Medic+Cepo (~90 of 594 teams at the projected floor, and convergence history says more), Medic+Gugnon (~85), Medic+Oliveira or Medic+Rebecki (~75 each). Carrying one of those pairs means starting the night sharing your entry with dozens of opponents before a punch lands. Also refused: both sides of Gugnon/Janicic on one team (one side always scores near zero), and three-plus hammers (the salary math below says the rest of that team is forced punts).
4. **The salary shape.** One hammer (~$9.4K) leaves about $8.1K per remaining spot — comfortable, the whole Good tier stays reachable. Two hammers (~$18.7K) leaves about $7.8K per spot — you can still afford one mid-priced piece plus dogs. Three hammers (~$27.9K) leaves $7.4K per spot — everything else is a dog, and the entry becomes exactly the chalk-plus-punts shape the field mass-produces. The four-hammer team does not exist under the cap. WHICH hammer an entry drops is the real decision, and the quiet favorites (Rakic/Milosevic) cost hammer money — anchoring one of them is a hammer slot spent on leverage.
5. **Two contests, two different answers.** The 588-person Flying Knee is top-heavy — the win is everything — so that single bullet wants the most contrarian live thesis: the quiet-favorite spine (e.g., a Rakic or Milosevic anchor) that only ~1 in 7 teams shares, because a shared win in a top-heavy field splits the prize. The 594-person Sprawl is balanced and gives you three bullets: three DISTINCT theses (your codified rule — never the same conviction core twice), e.g. one anchored on each different hammer, each with its own one-sentence reason it wins. Four entries answering four different questions; never one bet paid for four times.
6. **The pre-lock check.** Ask each finished lineup: (a) Does it carry at least one of the named quiet favorites — Rakic, Milosevic, Luciano, Urbina — or the Prepolec dart? (b) Is its average popularity per spot roughly in the winners' 31-40% band, not above? (c) Does it avoid carrying both Medic and Cepo (and both sides of any fight)? (d) Can every fighter on it WIN — no slot whose best case is points-in-a-loss (except a deliberate Cowan-style punt)? (e) Can you say in one sentence how this exact team wins its contest?

The one-line target: six fighters who can all win, ~30-40% average popularity, differentiated by one or two under-trusted quiet favorites — that is the exact shape of all three of your last logged contest winners, and it's the shape moklovin (beat you 2 of 5) runs while never needing the long shot.
