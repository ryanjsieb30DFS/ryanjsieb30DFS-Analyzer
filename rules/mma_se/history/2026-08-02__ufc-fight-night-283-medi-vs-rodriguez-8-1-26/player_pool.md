## The board, ranked

**1. Uros Medic** — $9,400, 42% owned (DailyFan; the article expects "the 40 percent range"), proj 84.5, ceiling 107.2. Both article writers rank him the number one play on the whole slate. He has the best odds on the card to win by a fast knockout (-375 to finish inside the distance — meaning the fight ends before the judges). His last three wins were all first-round finishes, and each scored more than 100 DraftKings points. The risk is real: the article says if he does not knock his man out early, he might not win at all — and 42% owned means about 2 of every 5 teams will have him, so his win is widely shared. — Core

**2. Mateusz Rebecki** — $9,600, 30% owned (DailyFan), proj 87.2, ceiling 99.0. He is the biggest betting favorite on the card at -700, which means the sportsbooks give him about an 84% chance to win. His path is wrestling: he takes opponents down about 3 times per 15 minutes and beats them up on the mat. The article calls his best-case night (his ceiling) "elite," and he averages 107 DraftKings points per win. The catch is his price — at $9,600 he squeezes the rest of your salary — and he absorbs a lot of punches, so he is hittable. — Core

**3. Michael Oliveira** — $9,200, 30% owned (DailyFan), proj 78.3, ceiling 97.7. The article calls him "a killer" who can out-strike almost anyone in this division. He is -150 to finish the fight early, and he scored 131 points in his Contender Series win. His unknown is wrestling defense — nobody has really tried to take him down yet. If his opponent just holds him against the cage or drags him down, the big score never comes. — Core

**4. Vlasto Cepo** — $9,100, 36% owned (DailyFan), proj 78.9, ceiling 103.4. For the first two minutes he throws full-power hooks nonstop, and 13 of his 14 career wins came in round one. If he wins, it is almost certainly the fast-knockout shape this scoring loves. But the article is blunt: after two minutes he is exhausted, and his ground game is close to nonexistent. He wins big early or he falls apart — and at 36% owned, more than 1 in 3 teams ride that gamble with you. — Core

**5. Aleksandar Rakic** — $9,300, 13% owned (DailyFan), proj 72.9, ceiling 89.6. This is the slate's biggest quiet-favorite gap: a 75% chance to win but only about 1 in 8 teams will have him. The crowd avoids him because he has lost four straight fights, so clicking his name feels bad. The article says there are real outcomes where he knocks out Tybura in two minutes — Tybura has been knocked out badly before. The bust side is just as real: if this turns into a slow staring contest, his last decision scored only 53 points. — Good · Leverage

**6. Nina Milosevic** — $9,500, 16% owned (DailyFan), proj 80.4, ceiling 93.6. She has an 80% chance to win but only about 1 of every 6 teams will play her — a big favorite the crowd is ignoring (leverage). Her opponent Cowan has been finished 4 times in 12 fights, and the article's pick is Milosevic by choke in round two. Her path to 90-100 points is dominating on the mat. The risk is price: at $9,500, a slow decision win would score too little to justify the cost. — Good · Leverage

**7. Gilbert Urbina** — $7,100, 15% owned (DailyFan), proj 31.4, ceiling 101.7. Both writers' favorite underdog — Gordo ranks him third on the entire slate. The plan is simple: survive Cepo's two-minute storm, then wrestle a man the article says has "no cardio, no ground game." Urbina averages nearly 3 takedowns per 15 minutes and is +315 to finish the fight early himself. The danger is obvious — he has been knocked out in round one in back-to-back fights, including by Medic — so he may not survive those first two minutes. — Good · Leverage

**8. Noah Gugnon** — $8,000, 34% owned (DailyFan), proj 58.3, ceiling 96.7. He has moved from underdog to slight betting favorite, and the article expects him to be the more popular side of this fight. He is the younger, better athlete, and when he gets on top his ground-and-pound (punching a grounded opponent) is vicious. Both sides of this fight historically put up huge scores in wins. At 34% owned — about 1 in 3 teams — you pay full crowd price for a near coin flip. — Good

**9. Milos Janicic** — $8,200, 26% owned (DailyFan), proj 55.6, ceiling 96.2. The other half of the same wild fight, at meaningfully lower popularity than Gugnon. He has 11 knockouts in 19 wins and actually has better odds to finish early (+120) than Gugnon does (+160). The article says it wants "a fair amount of action" on both sides and would split them nearly evenly. His flaw is defense — he stands still with his hands low, so he can get hurt too. — Good

**10. Robert Valentin** — $8,500, 25% owned (DailyFan), proj 64.4, ceiling 95.8. He is -120 to finish early against Todorovic, who the article says "gives up 97 points per loss" — meaning even his opponents' wins tend to be huge. Valentin is coming off a 102-point performance. The article picked him to win this fight. The worry is durability: he has been knocked out and out-wrestled before, so this could also turn into an ugly, even brawl. — Good

**11. Bogdan Grad** — $8,600, 23% owned (DailyFan), proj 69.0, ceiling 92.9. The two writers openly disagree here: Gordo ranks him first in the whole mid-price range, Brett ranks him seventh. He boxes at a high pace, chases takedowns hard (eight attempts in one recent fight), and the article says a finish means "110 or more." The catch is the fight is priced to go long (-140 to pass 2.5 rounds), and without a finish his score is strong but probably not slate-winning. — Okay

**12. Jovan Leka** — $8,700, 28% owned (DailyFan), proj 69.0, ceiling 95.0. A 6-foot-5 hometown heavyweight with a huge size edge over an opponent coming in on short notice and up a weight class. He wins minutes with jabs and heavy top control, and 6 of his 11 finishes came in round one. The article's worry: both guys are decent enough that the fight extends, and Leka has never been a fast-score machine — his best DWCS win was slow-paced. — Okay

**13. Stephanie Luciano** — $8,900, 13% owned (DailyFan; the article says she "could approach 10 percent"), proj 71.9, ceiling 86.7. A 74% favorite that only about 1 in 8 teams will play — quiet-favorite leverage. She just choked out Ravena Oliveira for 93 points, so the grappling path is real. But the article is skeptical: her most likely win is a striking decision that scores in the mid-60s, which does not pay at $8,900. She needs the ground game to show up, not just a win. — Okay · Leverage

**14. Daniel Rodriguez** — $6,800, 21% owned (DailyFan; the article guesses "that 20 percent range"), proj 30.4, ceiling 108.1 — the highest single ceiling number on the board. He is the classic main-event underdog pivot: a striker who lands over 7 significant strikes a minute against a favorite who has been dropped inside a minute multiple times. The article: "if he wins, he should crush." The equally honest line: "I'm not certain Rodriguez will last longer than two minutes here." — Okay

**15. Borislav Nikolic** — $8,400, 27% owned (DailyFan), proj 67.9, ceiling 93.2. A short-notice hometown debutant with a 94% finish rate and real size for the weight class. The article's catch is the shape of his wins: he often loses minutes early and finds his finishes late, which caps points. A knockout or a punch-then-choke (club-and-sub) is on the table at +155 to finish early. Volatile, not safe. — Okay

**16. Dusko Todorovic** — $7,700, 28% owned (DailyFan), proj 50.1, ceiling 96.2. The crowd's favorite underdog — 28% owned for a 42% chance to win, while the other side of his own fight (Valentin, 58% to win) costs less popularity. His ceiling is real: he averages 110 DraftKings points per win and is +200 to finish early. The strategy's call is to own him, but less than the crowd does (underweight) — the math favors the winning side of this coin flip. — Okay

**17. Mark Vologdin** — $7,800, 20% owned (DailyFan), proj 52.5, ceiling 93.8. The rare underdog whose losses still produce points: he lands nearly 7 strikes per minute and the article says he "could push 90 points even in a decision win." That volume gives him a floor most cheap dogs do not have. The problem is he absorbs 10 strikes per minute — the article stopped finding that cute — and the bigger Nikolic may simply wear him down. — Okay

**18. Ludovit Klein** — $8,800, 21% owned (DailyFan), proj 69.3, ceiling 89.0. A safe favorite (70% to win) whose problem is price versus points: his last three decisions scored 64, 86, and 89, and this fight is -140 to go the full three rounds. At $8,800 he needs a finish or submission to justify the cost, and he is only +215 to get one. The strategy's call: own some, but less than the crowd (underweight). — Okay

**19. Dennis Buzukja** — $7,600, 18% owned (DailyFan), proj 45.1, ceiling 91.3. On paper the second-most-likely underdog under $8,000 to win. His case is a single clean punch: Grad has now been knocked out twice, and Buzukja can hit. The article's faith is low — he has been out-landed badly in most fights and may be on his way out of the UFC — but at this price the knockout world is live. — Okay

**20. Kyle Prepolec** — $6,600, 7% owned (DailyFan), proj 28.7, ceiling 89.6. The board's only true under-10%-owned dart (a cheap long shot almost nobody plays). The article calls him "kind of sneaky to win": he was out-striking Drew Dober for two rounds last fight, and Rebecki absorbs enormous damage — over 300 significant strikes across his recent hard fights. If Prepolec lands the shock knockout at 7% owned, he decides the whole slate. The honest odds: he loses most of the time, which is why he costs $6,600. — Okay · Leverage

**21. Tofiq Musayev** — $7,400, 18% owned (DailyFan), proj 42.8, ceiling 92.7. A pure one-punch bet: 18 of his 23 career wins came by knockout, and he has real speed and power. The style matchup is the problem — Klein is durable, defensively sound, and has only been finished when pressured with pace, which is not how Musayev fights. Binary: the blast lands or the night is a zero. — Okay

**22. Jan Blachowicz** — $7,200, 17% owned (DailyFan), proj 41.0, ceiling 88.0. A former champion at a cheap price whose opponent has never beaten a good striker. The article thinks the line is too wide and he can win a competitive decision. The scoring problem: he produces low volume, the fight profiles slow, and the article would "bet against a knockout" — so even his win may not score much. Cheap salary help with a modest best night. — Okay

**23. Alexander Poppeck** — $7,500, 17% owned (DailyFan), proj 40.8, ceiling 93.7. A power counter-puncher with 12 first-round finishes and a 72% finish rate — one clean hook changes everything. The situation is the fade case: short notice, moving up a full weight class, against a much bigger man who will lean on him. The article calls him viable for the price but a low-end option. — Okay

**24. Oban Elliott** — $7,000, 14% owned (DailyFan), proj 33.2, ceiling 92.5. The article's named leverage against the Oliveira hype: nobody knows if Oliveira can stop a takedown, and Elliott will try. The doubt is that Elliott's own wrestling is not actually good, and he is only +650 to finish early. His realistic win is a grinding decision that may not score well. A thin path, but it attacks the field's favorite prospect. — Okay

**25. Hailey Cowan** — $6,700, 10% owned (DailyFan), proj 36.7, ceiling 85.8. The article's cheap-team filler case: she "could end up optimal in a loss with 30 points" — a minimum-price player you accept low points from to afford stars (a punt). Her physical clinch game can bank a few takedowns even while losing. The same article also calls her "god awful" as a fighter, so treat her as salary relief, not a winner. — Okay

**26. Marcin Tybura** — $6,900, 14% owned (DailyFan), proj 34.0, ceiling 84.1. The article only likes Tybura when he can wrestle, and it calls this a bad matchup for that: Rakic defends 85% of takedowns. Tybura has landed 1 knockdown in 58 UFC rounds — historically bad power for a heavyweight — so the random-knockout world is thinner than heavyweight variance suggests. Lowest ceiling among the underdogs, in a fight the article expects to be slow. — Fade

**27. Marina Spasic** — $7,300, 14% owned (DailyFan), proj 38.5, ceiling 84.9. Her best case is winning an ugly, close striking decision in front of the home crowd — and the article's own math says that win might only score about 65 points at $7,300. She is +900 to finish early, meaning almost nobody expects her to end the fight. The article's conclusion: "I'll generally be passing here." Even her win probably does not help you. — Fade

**28. Navajo Stirling** — $9,000, 23% owned (DailyFan), proj 71.8, ceiling 89.2. The strategy's clearest fade: a 74% favorite whose big score requires something that has never happened — Blachowicz has never been knocked out in round one in his entire career. The article expects an "incredibly slow" pace, notes Jan allows just 12.8 DraftKings points per 5 minutes, and says "if this fight hits round three I'd wager Stirling is dead on DraftKings." Paying 23% popularity — nearly 1 in 4 teams — for a historic first is the trap. He can win and still sink your entry. — Fade
