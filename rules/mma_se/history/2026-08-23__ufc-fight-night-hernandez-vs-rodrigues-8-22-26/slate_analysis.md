## The short version

This section is the whole slate in plain words, for someone who knows nothing about DFS.

1. This is a 13-fight UFC card with many fights that can end early, which is where big fantasy scores come from.
2. Points come from winning, and winning fast pays extra, so the biggest scores come from round-one finishes or takedown after takedown.
3. The crowd is piling onto the main event: more than half of all teams will have Anthony Hernandez, and most of the rest will have Gregory Rodrigues.
4. The decision that separates entries is the expensive slot: Lerryan Douglas, Anthony Wint and Marcio Barbosa are all "knockout early or bust" at about the same price.
5. A sharp fills that slot with the fighter the crowd ignores, Shanelle Dyer, who is just as likely to win but sits on only about 1 in 11 teams.
6. The biggest trap is a price shape, not a person: mid-priced fighters picked far more often than their projected points deserve, led by Chris Padilla in a coin-flip fight likely to go the distance.
7. All six fighters on a team must be able to WIN, because three months of results show the cheap slot decides the contest more than any star.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually matter on this slate. It states each edge and stops; the decisions are yours.

1. **The main event is where the field lives.** DailyFan has Hernandez at 56% and Rodrigues at 37%, so about 93 of every 100 teams carry one side.
2. **The consensus name will run hotter than projected.** The last three slates' storyline fighter landed ~15 points above projection, so Hernandez likely arrives near 70% (7 of 10 teams).
3. **Anchor-Equivalence (same bet, different fighter):** Wint ($9,900, 24%), Barbosa ($9,800, 25%) and Douglas ($9,300, 38%) are near-identical "win by early knockout" favorites; Dyer ($9,700, 9%) is the same tier at one-third the ownership.
4. **The article and the projection disagree on the main event:** Brett picks Rodrigues by round-2 TKO (low confidence); DailyFan gives Hernandez a 66% win chance.
5. **Trap-shaped prices this slate (ownership ahead of projected points):** Chris Padilla 34% (16 ranks ahead), Wes Schultz 28% (10), Kennedy Nzechukwu 28% (9), Vitor Petrino 24% (8), Nasrat Haqparast 23% (7), Mason Jones 28% (6).
6. **Where your opponents go (history, not player quality):** in your past Flying Knee and Sprawl fields the crowd lands on ~8 names at ~36–42% each, and the losing half keeps buying 25%+-owned fighters priced $8,000–$10,000 (8 of 12 and 6 of 6 past traps). This slate's fit: Hernandez, Douglas, Padilla, De Ridder, Dorsainvil, Jones, McVey, Nzechukwu.
7. **Duplication (teams that copy each other):** the field pairs Hernandez + Douglas in ~21% of teams, about 304 of 1,426 in the Clinch — closer to 27% (~380) after the convergence bump. Hernandez + Rodrigues is two sides of one fight and cannot both win.
8. **The sub-10% converters (low-owned fighters who actually WIN):** Dyer (9%, 85% win chance) is the only one; Chatman, Kuse, Chaves and Reed win 13–18% of the time, and under $7,700 only Rodrigues, Emmers and Mederos have a real win path.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section compares how the crowd will build against how a pro will build.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical team carries Hernandez (or Rodrigues) plus Douglas, then fills the middle with the popular $7,800–$9,200 names: De Ridder, Padilla, Dorsainvil, Jones, McVey, Nzechukwu. Past Flying Knee fields were only 65% unique, and the most-copied team appeared 22 times.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The observed pro in your contests (moklovin: 14 slates, ~30% ownership per slot, a sub-5% piece in 29% of lineups) anchors on the popular converting favorites too. His separation comes from WHICH mid-priced fighters win, plus one low-owned piece (Dyer 9%, Emmers 15%), while refusing the 25%+-owned fighters whose ownership outruns their projection (Padilla, Schultz, Nzechukwu).

**The gap.** The field pays 38% ownership for Douglas's early-knockout profile; a sharp gets the same 85% win chance and a 97-point ceiling (DailyFan) from Dyer at 9%. The field also spends 57% combined ownership on the Padilla/Haqparast coin flip that is -190 to go the distance.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes, in order, on this slate. Suggestions here are in the sharp's voice; the picks are yours.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors in the main event, because Hernandez's 124.6-per-win history is the slate's true ceiling, and hedges the coin flip across entries (Rodrigues in one). The expensive twins — Douglas 38%, Wint 24%, Barbosa 25%, Dyer 9% — are ONE bet; a sharp picks the side the field is not on.
2. **The leverage piece.** It must be able to WIN (the last three slates' winners carried zero losing fighters). Candidates: Dyer 9%, Emmers 15%, Gaziev 22%, Haqparast 23%. moklovin carries a sub-5% piece in about 3 of 10 lineups, so on 5 bullets that is one or two — and Chatman or Kuse (5%) fit only the Clinch's top-heavy payout.
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Hernandez + Douglas together means sharing the entry with ~300–380 opponents in the Clinch before the first bell. Padilla + Nzechukwu together means two trap-shaped prices in one team.
4. **The salary shape.** Hernandez ($8,600) + Douglas ($9,300) leaves $32,100 for four slots, an average of $8,025 — the exact tier the losing half of past fields keeps buying. Swapping Douglas for Dyer costs $400 more; swapping him for Emmers frees $2,400 and pays for Wint. The real decision is whether the second expensive slot goes to Douglas, Dyer, or nobody.
5. **Three contests, three different entries.** Flying Knee (784): the highest-conviction converting six. Sprawl (594, 3-Max): the three entries split the main event and the expensive twins, never sharing two coin-flip slots (8/2 lesson: shared coin flips, not shared anchors, kill a block). Clinch (1,426, top-heavy): the win is everything, so a true dart (Chatman or Kuse) belongs here and nowhere else.
6. **The big-field attack.** No 20-Max or 150-Max is declared, so this step is skipped.
7. **The pre-lock check.** Can all six answer "how does he WIN?" — including the cheapest slot? Is there one fighter under 15% ownership with a real path? Is average ownership per slot near 30% (moklovin runs 29.8%), not 40%+? Does the team avoid Hernandez + Douglas together? Is every name written in full?

Target, in one line: six fighters who can all win, ~30% average ownership per slot, one under-trusted converter (Dyer or Emmers), all five entries unique — the structure moklovin has beaten you with 5 times in 14 meetings.
