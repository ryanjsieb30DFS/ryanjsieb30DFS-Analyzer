# MMA slate strategy — Noche UFC, Saturday 9/12/26

## The short version

This section is the whole slate in plain words, for someone who knows nothing about fantasy fighting.

1. This is a 13-fight UFC card, and you win by picking six fighters who win their fights.
2. Points come almost entirely from winning, and the biggest scores come from knockouts, submissions, or wrestlers who land takedown after takedown.
3. The crowd is piling onto three fighters, and about half of all teams will have Tommy McMillen.
4. The crowd is also betting on underdogs in four mid-priced fights, so the favorites in those same fights are lightly picked.
5. The one decision that separates entries is how many of the three crowd favorites to hold, because holding all three forces cheap long shots into the last slots.
6. A sharp player takes one big-money favorite the crowd is skipping, then fills the middle with favorites the crowd is betting against.
7. The biggest trap is paying top dollar for a favorite whose win only scores about 70 points.
8. Every fighter under $7,500 is a long shot, so the cheap slot is where most lineups die.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually matter on this card. It states the edge and stops. It gives no orders.

1. **Same win chance, half the crowd.** DailyFan has Gantt at 79% to win and 43% picked, Bahamondes at 81% and 25%, Belgaroui at 86% and 20%.
2. **Anchor twins (two favorites the crowd treats as the same bet).** Silva ($9,300, 42% picked) and Gantt ($9,400, 43%) are near-identical: both about 78% to win, both project near 88 points.
3. **The crowd bets the underdog in four fights.** Elliott 27% picked vs Chairez 24%; Garcia 30% vs Rongzhu 23%; Blaydes 26% equals Acosta 26%; Morales 32% vs Moreno 18% on a coin-flip.
4. **McMillen will run hotter than 48%.** He is the narrative fighter (168 points last fight, "fight of the year" per Technical Tim); the framework says one such name beats projection by about 15 points every card.
5. **The field pairs McMillen + Gantt in ~21% of lineups, about 245 of the 1,189 Clinch teams.** McMillen + Silva is about 240 more. Carrying either pair means sharing the entry before a punch lands.
6. **The article and the sheet disagree on Elliott and Garcia.** Brett picks both underdogs to win; DailyFan gives them 34% and 41%. That gap is where the crowd's dog bets come from.
7. **Your SE opponents piled onto Tommy McMillen in 2 of your 10 past SE fields.** That is a map of where they go, not a read on him. Their losing-half price shape: 47 of 67 traps were 25%+ picked, 46 of 80 sat in the $8,000–$10,000 tier.
8. **Trap-shaped prices this slate (picked ahead of projection):** Rafa Garcia (+10 ranks), Manon Fiorot (+7), Sean King III (+6), Rong Rongzhu (+6), Alexa Grasso (+6), Curtis Blaydes (+5). The ledger says this screen went 2-for-6 one week and 5-for-6 the next, so it argues nothing on its own.
9. **The four cheapest fighters are pure long shots.** Salikhov (8% picked, 19% to win), Santos (7%, 14%), Klose (9%, 21%), Aldrich (9%, 29%); no sub-$7,200 fighter is better than 3-in-10 to win.
10. **Five entries share a card, so the set is the bet.** With three of the crowd's anchors at 42–48% picked, any anchor in 4 or 5 of your 5 entries turns the set into one bet made five times.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows how the crowd will build and how a sharp will build, so you can see the gap.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry carries McMillen ($8,600) plus one or both of Gantt and Silva, then Sean King III at 38% picked and Rahiki at 36%. Its last slot is a cheap underdog it hopes scores in a loss: Delgado at 21% picked, or Elliott and Garcia as "value" dogs. That is about 40% of the crowd on every slot, and the McMillen + Gantt + Silva trio alone lands together in roughly 100 of the 1,189 Clinch lineups before any fourth name.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The observed pro in your contests, moklovin, runs about 30% picked per slot, carries a low-picked piece in about 1 of 4 lineups, and holds the top anchor in about half his lineups. On this card that means one anchor from the 75%+ favorites the crowd skips (Bahamondes 25%, Belgaroui 20%), or one of the Silva/Gantt twins but never both. His middle is the under-picked favorites: Chairez 24%, Rongzhu 23%, Acosta 26%, Fiorot 16%. His cheap slot is a fighter who can win, not one who scores in a loss. He refuses to hold all three of McMillen, Gantt and Silva, because that forces three long shots into the other slots.

**The gap.** The field pays 42–48% ownership for a 78% win chance in the $9,300–$9,400 tier and bets underdogs in the middle. A sharp buys the same 80% win chance at 20–25% ownership (Bahamondes, Belgaroui) and takes the favorites the crowd is betting against in the middle, at 23–26% ownership. Same number of likely winners, roughly half the co-owners.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks the decisions a sharp makes, in order, applied to this card. It suggests in the sharp's voice. It names no roster.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors from the 75%+ favorites and picks the one the crowd is NOT paying for. Candidates: Bahamondes (81% to win, 25% picked), Belgaroui (86%, 20%), or ONE of the twins Gantt (79%, 43%) and Silva (77%, 42%). The twins are one bet: a sharp holds one of them, never both, and holds Bahamondes or Belgaroui in at least one entry.
2. **The leverage piece.** It comes from the under-picked favorites in the middle, not from the cheap tier: Chairez (24%), Rongzhu (23%), Fiorot (16%), Moreno (18%). The pro envelope is about 30% picked per slot with a sub-10% piece in roughly 1 of 4 lineups, so across 5 entries one Klose or Aldrich is plenty, and none is fine.
3. **What the field duplicates.** McMillen + Gantt lands in ~21% of lineups (about 245 of the 1,189 Clinch teams) and McMillen + Silva in ~20% (about 240); with McMillen likely at 60%+ real ownership, both pairs run closer to 1 in 4. A pair count is information, not a rule. The refusal on this card is the TRIPLE: McMillen + Gantt + Silva costs $27,300, leaving $22,700 for three slots, an average of $7,567, which is the long-shot tier. So a sharp holds at most two of the three, and when breaking a pair drops the more-picked half (McMillen) rather than the twin.
4. **The salary shape.** One $9,000+ anchor leaves $40,600 for five slots, about $8,120 each: that fits the $7,500–$8,999 tier where the win chances live. Two $9,000+ anchors leave $31,300 for four, about $7,825 each: still fine, but the cheap slot must be a fighter who can win (Rahiki 40%, Rosas 32%, Moreno 49%), not one who scores in a loss. Salary stays at $48,500 or more; no more than one fighter under $7,200.
5. **Three contests, three different entries.** The 784-team Flying Knee pays $2K to first, so it is the most top-heavy: that bullet is the most differentiated one, with the under-picked anchor and zero crowd pairs, because a copied roster splits first place. The 1,189-team Clinch is the biggest field: that bullet is where the second twin goes, so the two SEs differ on an anchor. The 594-team 3-Max is three distinct theses: each entry answers a different question (which twin, which side of McMillen/Rahiki, which under-picked favorite), and no two entries share more than one coin-flip slot.
6. **The big-field attack.** Not applicable: no 20-Max or 150-Max contest is declared.
7. **The pre-lock check.** Does every fighter have a one-sentence way to WIN his fight, cheap slot included? Does the entry hold at most one of Silva and Gantt? Does it hold at most two of McMillen, Gantt and Silva? Does it avoid both sides of any one fight? Across the five entries, is every anchor in three entries or fewer, and does at least one entry carry Bahamondes or Belgaroui?

Target in one line: moklovin's envelope, about 30% picked per slot, the top anchor in about half the set, a low-picked piece in about 1 of 4 entries.
