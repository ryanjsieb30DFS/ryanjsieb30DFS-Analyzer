**1. Mateusz Gamrot** — $7,900, 53% owned (DailyFan), proj 67.0, ceiling 107.9. Both writers rank him the #1 play on the whole card. He wins with takedowns — Brett says 8 to 10 are possible across five rounds. He averages 105 DraftKings points per win and has reached 131. The catch is the crowd, not the fighter: 53% owned means more than half of all teams will have him. And by DailyFan's own number he still loses this fight 45% of the time. — Core

**2. Yadier del Valle** — $9,600, 34% owned (DailyFan), proj 91.8, ceiling 103.7. The safest win on the card: 86% to win, about 6 times out of 7. Brett calls him "safer and more likely to win ITD than everyone else at the top end" — ITD means winning inside the distance, before the final bell. His best realistic night (his ceiling) is capped, though: Brett says "100 is more likely than 120." A quick two-minute submission win would actually score modestly for his big price. — Core

**3. Juliana Miller** — $8,900, 29% owned (DailyFan), proj 74.1, ceiling 95.6. Gordo's #1 in the top price tier and both writers' #3 overall. Brett: "total ground domination and 110 point scores in her wheelhouse." She has one of the best early-finish prices on the card, against an opponent who is 0-3 in the UFC. The risk is real too: Brett admits "she could definitely bust," because she is very hittable on the feet. — Good

**4. Carol Foro** — $9,300, 14% owned (DailyFan), proj 71.5, ceiling 93.9. The same needs-a-finish profile as the popular favorites, at a third of their ownership — 14% is about 1 team in 7. Brett: same chance to win early "as the others in this range, but she should be far lower owned. Good pivot play." Her path is heavy hands: 7.8 strikes landed per minute, and Brett says a round-2 stoppage is on the table. The risk is the ground — her only pro loss came on the mat, and Canuto is an elite grappler. — Good · Leverage

**5. Alexia Thainara** — $8,800, 32% owned (DailyFan), proj 74.6, ceiling 92.6. She wins with takedowns plus holding her opponent down (control time, which DraftKings pays well). Her last three wins scored 119, 103 and 106. Lemos gives up soft takedowns, so Brett says 3-4 takedowns and 10 minutes on top are on the table again. His worry: some regression, "closer to 3 takedowns and 90 points," and she rarely finishes early. — Good

**6. Steven Asplund** — $9,000, 36% owned (DailyFan), proj 78.4, ceiling 96.6. Monster punch volume for a heavyweight — 170 significant strikes in a recent win. His opponent gasses out badly, so Brett predicts a round-2 stoppage after Pat tires. The catch: 36% owned makes him the most-picked expensive favorite, and Pat wants to slow the fight down early, which dents the volume path. — Good

**7. Ty Miller** — $9,200, 33% owned (DailyFan), proj 80.0, ceiling 96.1. Brett calls him "a boom or bust boxer." The boom: a war that ends in a brutal knockout and a smash score. The bust: he lands 80-100 strikes, wins a decision, and scores only in the mid-70s. He is 4 inches taller with 5 more inches of reach than a very hittable Goff, and the public is chasing his 125-point debut — Brett says "that's what the public will do." — Good

**8. Diyar Nurgozhay** — $8,400, 29% owned (DailyFan), proj 63.4, ceiling 96.4. Both writers like his knockout power: he is 6-0 when it ends by knockout, against an opponent knocked out in all three of his UFC losses. The fight is priced to end early. It is binary in both directions — both of Nurgozhay's UFC losses came by submission on the mat, exactly where Lopes is best. Brett's other worry is a slow start and a long sweat for a mid-round finish. — Good

**9. Miles Johns** — $8,600, 16% owned (DailyFan), proj 60.8, ceiling 86.3. A clear favorite the public is skipping — Brett expects "low teens" ownership and says "he'll be contrarian." The path: he "can occasionally pop off for 8 takedown attempts and that could at least be a path to scoring" into the 90s. The risk: he has not topped 75 DraftKings points in eight straight fights, so a careful decision win scores small. — Good · Leverage

He averages 98 DraftKings points per win over his last ten wins, and can score by knockout, by grappling, or by pace. The risk: he is 41 years old and was knocked out cold in his last fight. — Good
