## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

**How the FIELD will play it.** The article itself predicts the shape: "The easiest thing to do is to play stars/scrubs lineups which bank on some of these big guns to hit." Expect the mass of lineups to jam the $9.2K+ tier — Dulatov (32%), Patterson (32%), Tuchalov (29%), Hussein (29%), Ankalaev (27%) — pair two of them together (Walker+Patterson, Walker+Dulatov, Patterson+Dulatov are the top co-occurring combos at ~10-11% each), add the chalkiest mid-priced favorite Walker (34%) and the popular debutant Saidov (29%), then fill the cheap slots with the "safe" names (Rzepecki, Petersen). That is a high-ownership, finish-dependent build that shares its spine with a hundred-plus other entries.

**How a SHARP will play it.** With no observed shark-envelope numbers for this slate, the target comes from the reverse-engineered MMA sharp profile: roughly **~30% average ownership per roster slot** (MMA is a chalky-ownership sport — the sharks are NOT contrarian on ownership) but with **a sub-5%-or-low-owned leverage piece in ~15% of their lineups**, which is the exact thing your own MMA entries have carried **0% of the time** (same-slate head-to-head vs the sharks). The sharp still anchors an elite finisher — Dulatov or Tuchalov, the correct anchor — but differentiates below it with a low-owned *winning* favorite (Kuniev 16%, Zaynukov 21%, Erceg 21%) rather than a second chalk anchor, and refuses to share the field's top chalk pair.

**The gap on THIS slate.** The field pays ~30%+ ownership for its second and third anchors (a second $9K name, Walker at 34%, Saidov at 29%); the sharp gets a comparable ceiling at 16-21% ownership from Kuniev, Zaynukov, or Erceg — the same win-side equity the crowd is under-rating because those names lack the narrative or the box-score. That is precisely where "avoid the field, follow the sharp" lives this week: same anchor at the top, a low-owned converter where the field spends up on a second chalk favorite. Your process trend makes this the priority — your leverage capture read 0% on the most recent measured slate, and `own_per_slot` was your biggest structural gap vs the pros in 2 of the last 5 slates. The archetype to copy is **moklovin** (won the 6/14 slate): rides the correct elite anchor, then lands the low-owned converter the field skipped — your edge vs that build is carrying the leverage piece at all.

**How a sharp USES the key players (usage guidance — you pick the players; this is how a sharp holds them):**

- **Islam Dulatov ($9,800, 32%)** — the sharp treats him as the correct top anchor and is fine at or slightly under the field's 32%, because at -650 ITD with a ~110 ceiling he is the cleanest smash on the board. The construction cost: anchoring the most expensive fighter at $9.8K forces cheaper value below him, and his natural salary partners are the same $9.4-9.7K names (Patterson/Tuchalov/Hussein) the field jams — so a Dulatov build has to find its uniqueness from the value tier, not from a second $9K anchor.

- **Magomed Tuchalov ($9,700, 29%)** — the sharp uses him as the *pivot* off Dulatov (the anchor-equivalence alternative): same finish ceiling, 3 points less owned, and he wins the leverage battle if his side lands the finish while the Dulatov-heavy field misses. A sharp will rarely carry both in one bullet — that is $19.5K on two boom/bust finishers and a shared-with-the-field construction.

- **Valter Walker ($8,800, 34% — the chalkiest play)** — a sharp comes in **underweight relative to the field's 34%**, because the ownership is a finish-or-bust profile (Brett: points come "directly from the finish") priced like a safe play. Using him means accepting a heel-hook-or-bust slot, and a sharp refuses to also carry the Walker+Patterson / Walker+Dulatov chalk pair on top of it — if Walker is in, the rest of the build must be uniquely his.

- **Sam Patterson ($9,400, 32%)** — the sharp holds him at or below field ownership and knows the construction implication: at $9.4K his median is a ~94-101 decision (career-high 25 strikes landed, only topped 101 once), so he only pays off on an early finish. A sharp who uses him leans into the finish thesis and does NOT pair him with the other two chalk anchors, since the top co-occurring pairs all include him.

- **Rizvan Kuniev ($9,000, 16%)** — this is the sharp's differentiation slot: a -335 converting favorite at half the ownership of the chalk tier, with a 110-point ceiling in both DWCS wins. Using him at 16% is where a sharp buys ceiling the field is paying 30%+ for elsewhere; the risk a sharp accepts is his boom/bust HW floor (70.8 and 51 in his two recent wins if it goes the distance).

- **Magomed Zaynukov ($9,200, 21%)** — a sharp uses him as a low-owned volume ceiling: he just posted 138 on DWCS, is a point-striker with an 80-strike decision floor AND +150 ITD upside, and at 21% he is under-owned for a -275 favorite. The construction value is real — you get a top-of-board ceiling without paying top-of-board ownership.

- **The top chalk combo (Walker + Patterson, ~10.9%):** a sharp carries **at most one side**, never both, and if forced to use one, makes every other slot in that lineup a low-owned converter so the build isn't a near-duplicate of ~130 other entries.

## How to approach the slate

Both contests are top-heavy Single Entries — the win is everything, so build each bullet to a maximum-ceiling thesis, not to cash. This is small-field SE: with one entry per contest, each lineup must be a complete, distinct thesis, and per the framework the two SEs should **differ on at least one conviction anchor** (do not run the same Dulatov+Tuchalov core in both — if that finish tier misses, both die identically). The 490-entry Flying Knee can lean slightly more chalk with one quality leverage swing; the 1,189-entry Clinch needs genuine differentiation across two axes.

The winning shape here is six *winning* fighters anchored by a correct elite finisher (Dulatov or Tuchalov), with the differentiation carried by a **low-owned converting favorite the field under-trusts** — Kuniev (16%), Zaynukov (21%), or Erceg (21%) — rather than by a second chalk anchor or a dead KO-or-bust dart. That directly closes your two recurring gaps: the sharp-envelope target is roughly **~30% average ownership per slot with at least one sub-5%-to-low-owned leverage piece in most bullets** (the pros carry one ~15% of the time; your MMA entries have carried one 0% of the time). Ownership discipline is already shark-level for you — the edge to close is *which* low-owned play you choose and whether you carry one at all. Follow the moklovin archetype: correct anchor at the top, the low-owned converter the field skipped underneath, every bullet unique.
