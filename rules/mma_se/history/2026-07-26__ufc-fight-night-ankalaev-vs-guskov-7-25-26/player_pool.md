1. **Islam Dulatov** — $9,800, 32% (DailyFan), proj 93.6, ceiling 105.9 — The cleanest ceiling on the board: he is roughly an 87% favorite to finish the fight (-650 to end it inside the distance) against a "failed prospect" who has been knocked out and submitted repeatedly, and Brett names a realistic 110-point score outright off a recent 108-point win. The only real caveat is the tag — at the most expensive price on the slate he forces you to save salary everywhere else, and if he wins by a slow submission rather than a blitz he can score 105 and still miss the top score. — Core

2. **Magomed Tuchalov** — $9,700, 29% (DailyFan), proj 92.3, ceiling 105.0 — A 100% career finish rate with every single finish coming in the first two rounds, and he is about an 82% favorite to end it early (-800 inside the distance, -175 to win in round one) against Ribeiro, who has been finished in the first round in three straight fights. Gordon Clark calls him one of the two best marks on the slate alongside Dulatov. His only risk is a fight that reaches the later rounds — something he has literally never experienced — so a decision is uncharted and lower-scoring for him. — Core

3. **Magomed Zaynukov** — $9,200, 21% (DailyFan), proj 75.7, ceiling 92.0 — This is the best low-owned leverage play among the real favorites: he just landed 235 strikes for 138 fantasy points on the Contender Series, so even a normal high-volume decision reaches the 80s, and his +150 line to finish adds a knockout path on top. Brett flatly says he "will likely provide some leverage against Rzepecki," and at 21% he is clearly under-owned for a fighter favored around -275. The risk is that a quieter decision (his 80-strike floor) scores well but not spectacularly. — Good · Leverage

4. **Abdul Hussein** — $9,300, 29% (DailyFan), proj 81.5, ceiling 96.8 — A well-rounded debut favorite (roughly -550) whom Brett says "could even be prioritized ahead of the super expensive names" precisely because he costs less than Dulatov and Tuchalov, and if he wins by finish "he's probably going to put up a strong score." He is about a -185 favorite to finish. The catch is that he is finish-dependent at the price — Brett worries a bit about him winning in the mid-rounds, and a competitive decision leaves him short of optimal at $9.3K. — Good

5. **Rizvan Kuniev** — $9,000, 16% (DailyFan), proj 71.2, ceiling 89.9 — The prime low-owned converting favorite: a roughly -335 pick who scored 110 in both of his Contender Series wins, against a wrestler (Fortune) whose durability is shaky and who "immediately gets reversed and gives up his back." Brett frames him as "more of a contrarian target this week… he may honestly be outright contrarian" at the lowest ownership in the $9K range. The risk is real heavyweight boom-or-bust — in his two recent decision-style wins he scored just 70.8 and 51, so he essentially needs the knockout to pay off the price. — Good · Leverage

6. **Sam Patterson** — $9,400, 32% (DailyFan), proj 80.0, ceiling 96.4 — A heavy favorite (around -500) who averages 100 points per win with four of his five UFC fights ending in round one, against Ponzinibbio, who has been knocked out four times and is "getting hurt more and more." The ceiling case is a quick finish worth 110+. The structural risk is a hard ceiling cap at the price: his career high is only 25 strikes landed and he has topped 101 points just once, so anything past round one likely lands him around 94-101 — a lot to pay $9.4K for. — Good

7. **Magomed Ankalaev** — $9,500, 27% (DailyFan), proj 84.3, ceiling 99.1 — The safest favorite on the card (roughly -600) with multiple paths to a win, but the ceiling is the problem: he averages only 90.6 points per win, has topped 100 just three times in twelve wins, and each of those came on a first-round knockout he is only +285 to get. Brett also thinks the public may spread off him, pushing him "into the mid-20s" or lower — so he can be a quietly lower-owned anchor, but he is a decision-grinder without an elite top end at a top-tier price. — Okay

8. **Axel Sola** — $8,600, 26% (DailyFan), proj 67.3, ceiling 92.1 — A well-rounded, gritty favorite (around -200, +125 to finish) whom Gordon Clark picks to win, describing him as a "DAWG" with great cardio who breaks opponents late — and Bonfim has been finished in all six of his career losses when pressured. If Sola gets the late finish he can return huge value on his price. The likelier outcome is a competitive striking-and-clinch decision that scores in the 80s rather than 100+. — Okay

9. **Abubakar Vagaev** — $8,900, 23% (DailyFan), proj 72.2, ceiling 89.9 — An elite top-control wrestler (around -230) and a 19-1 decision fighter whose path is pinning Izagakhmaev — a 1.33-strike-per-minute striker with no volume — and grinding out a clear decision. The knock, stated plainly by Gordon Clark, is a 21% career finish rate: he wins minutes but rarely finishes, so his ceiling is capped at a control-heavy decision (needs 3-4 takedowns and heavy control to hit even 90) unless he gets a rare stoppage. — Okay

10. **Nurullo Aliev** — $8,500, 28% (DailyFan), proj 70.1, ceiling 89.7 — A grappling-floor favorite (around -220) who wins by mixing takedowns and control against the hittable, action-fighter Davis. The problem Technical Tim flags is a very wide and capped scoring range — his three UFC decisions scored 64.5, 78 and 91.7 — so a mid-80s win is more likely than a 100+ ceiling, and his lack of offensive output makes even the win uncertain in points. — Okay

11. **Dustin Jacoby** — $8,700, 21% (DailyFan), proj 62.3, ceiling 89.4 — A leverage-friendly favorite (around -170, +160 to finish) coming off back-to-back knockouts of 107 and 99 points, and the article explicitly calls him a "leverage target" with early-KO upside at only 21% ownership. The risk is that Saidov is not a straightforward finishing opponent — an extended, competitive striking fight is likely, in which case Jacoby's ceiling shrinks toward a decision. — Okay · Leverage

12. **Steve Erceg** — $8,200, 21% (DailyFan), proj 56.9, ceiling 86.4 — The leverage side of a true coin-flip (the fight moved to a pick'em): Brett says "he projects poorly, but I think he'll end up optimal more often than he's owned," and his path is grappling-dominating the fading power-striker Temirov to a win that can "exceed 10x and contend for the optimal" (return well over ten times his salary in points). The risk is the pick'em itself — Temirov's early speed and power genuinely threaten to win the first round or hurt him. — Okay · Leverage

13. **Valter Walker** — $8,800, 34% (DailyFan), proj 67.8, ceiling 97.5 — The single most-owned fighter on the slate, riding a four-fight heel-hook streak (scores of 98/122/97/111), and at 34% the field is paying full price for a finish-or-nothing profile — Brett notes "almost all his DK points are coming directly from the finish." The ceiling is real if the leg lock lands early, but there is no decision-scoring floor: if the hook fails and the fight extends, Technical Tim thinks the better-cardio, better-wrestling Petersen can pound him out. High ceiling, highest ownership, zero floor. — Okay

14. **Ramazan Temirov** — $8,000, 28% (DailyFan), proj 60.4, ceiling 93.3 — A fast-twitch power striker in a pick'em who is essentially knockout-or-bust from a fantasy view: Brett says "a 100 point win from Temirov is on the table," but "if he doesn't score 100 points, Temirov will likely score closer to 60, and obviously less in a loss." His path is winning the early exchanges and hurting Erceg before he fades. The risk is his own cardio — he tires and gives away the later rounds. — Okay

15. **Thomas Petersen** — $7,400, 27% (DailyFan), proj 40.3, ceiling 89.3 — The cheap leverage side against the chalkiest fighter on the slate: Technical Tim thinks Petersen can stop Walker's heel hook and "wrestle box to a clean decision," and he already scored 94 points doing exactly that (wrestling and pounding out) against Pat. He is +170 to win and +375 to finish. The risk is getting heel-hooked in the first minute — the whole fight hinges on whether he survives Walker's early leg-lock attack. — Okay

16. **Muhammad Saidov** — $7,500, 29% (DailyFan), proj 51.4, ceiling 94.4 — A UFC debutant drawing surprisingly heavy ownership (29%) largely because the slate is "lacking easy underdogs," in what both writers expect to be a competitive striking fight with Jacoby. His path is a volume-and-power decision or an upset knockout. The concern is that he has faced poor competition and has no proven UFC ceiling, so the ownership outruns the evidence — usable, but an over-owned unknown. — Okay

17. **Ismael Bonfim** — $7,600, 26% (DailyFan), proj 46.6, ceiling 86.7 — A skilled boxer (+170 to win) with an 80-point decision floor in his two career decisions and knockout power if he controls the range against Sola. Gordon Clark picks Sola but concedes Bonfim "probably wins a comfortable 15 minute boxing affair" if it stays a pure striking match. The risk is his well-documented fragility — he has been finished in all six career losses when a hard pace is put on him, and Sola is built to grind. — Okay

18. **Mike Davis** — $7,700, 26% (DailyFan), proj 47.0, ceiling 88.6 — A fun, high-pace action fighter (+185) whose path is out-striking the low-output grappler Aliev, who "doesn't absorb much offense" and may not control him. Technical Tim notes the fight is likely to be competitive rather than a Aliev grind-fest. The risk is exactly that grind — if Aliev lands 3-4 takedowns and controls, Davis loses the minutes and the score. — Okay

19. **Saygid Izagakhmaev** — $7,300, 18% (DailyFan), proj 43.1, ceiling 93.6 — A live underdog (+195) with genuine finishing power and length — Gordon Clark says he is "the better fight finisher and moment winner in this matchup" and has a 73% career finish rate. At 18% ownership his finish equity is under-owned. The problem is stark: he lands only 1.33 strikes per minute, so there is essentially no decision-scoring floor — if he doesn't find the finish, "I just don't see him winning this fight" against the control wrestler Vagaev. — Okay · Leverage

20. **Bogdan Guskov** — $6,700, 21% (DailyFan), proj 32.2, ceiling 103.2 — The highest underdog ceiling on the board (103.2) in the main event: a power striker (+425) with round-one-to-two knockout ability against a former champion who is himself coming off a knockout loss, and Brett admits it's "hard to cap situations like this with Ankalaev coming off a KO loss." At 21% and $6.7K he is a cheap, high-ceiling dart. But it is pure boom-or-bust — he is a mediocre defensive boxer who "gets hit a good amount," and if he can't land early he loses clearly. — Okay · Leverage

21. **Santiago Ponzinibbio** — $6,800, 16% (DailyFan), proj 30.8, ceiling 89.4 — A low-owned upset flier (+385) whose case is entirely that Patterson is "hittable" and has been knocked out four times: Technical Tim says the best version of Ponz "could probably outstrike Patterson or maybe even hurt him," and Brett notes he "carries leverage against Patterson" at low ownership. At 16% he is a genuine sub-owned leverage dog. The likeliest outcome, though, is the 39-year-old getting clipped and finished — his durability is fading. — Okay · Leverage

22. **Damian Rzepecki** — $7,000, 24% (DailyFan), proj 50.8, ceiling 95.5 — An undefeated newcomer (+235) with a pressure-and-takedown path who "could score 35 in a loss and maybe be optimal if other dogs also lose," giving him more of a scoring floor than the pure-dart dogs. Brett expects him to be a popular stars-and-scrubs punt (he's the highest-owned dog here at 24%, so the least leverage). The problem is the matchup — Brett thinks he "ultimately gets outstruck en route to a clear decision" against the better point-striker Zaynukov. — Okay

23. **Tyrell Fortune** — $7,200, 21% (DailyFan), proj 37.5, ceiling 86.5 — A wrestling-based underdog (+275) whose entire path is landing takedowns on Kuniev — but Kuniev defended 93% of takedowns from Blaydes and Almeida, and Fortune's own submission grappling "looks bad" (he lands takedowns and "immediately gets reversed and gives up his back"), so he can't capitalize even when he wrestles. Brett calls this "a bad matchup on paper." A live-but-limited dart whose most realistic path is largely closed off. — Fade

24. **Cody Gibson** — $6,900, 14% (DailyFan), proj 35.2, ceiling 89.7 — A tough, experienced grappler taking the fight on two weeks' notice (+400, +700 to finish) against a debutant with untested takedown defense — the theoretical path is exposing Hussein on the mat. At 14% he is the lowest-owned play in his price range. But Technical Tim leans toward Hussein "completely shutting down the grappling of Gibson," and the 38-year-old was brutally knocked out in his last fight; short notice plus a likely-neutralized path makes this a dart to avoid. — Fade

25. **Brendson Ribeiro** — $6,500, 6% (DailyFan), proj 20.4, ceiling 98.0 — This is one of the two sub-10%-owned high-ceiling names the bundle flags, so it must be surfaced: he has a 94% career finish rate and real one-punch power, giving a genuine puncher's-chance knockout ceiling (98) at rock-bottom 6% ownership — the kind of play that can decide a top-heavy contest from nowhere if it lands. But the case against is overwhelming: "zero defense inside the Octagon, NONE," finished in the first round in three straight fights and roughly a 12% chance to win, so the overwhelmingly likely outcome is an early loss for zero points. Per the framework's leverage screen, a sub-10% dog carrying under ~15% win equity is a dart, not the slate's real leverage — hence Fade despite the ceiling. — Fade

26. **Wellington Turman** — $6,400, 5% (DailyFan), proj 19.5, ceiling 89.3 — The other bundle-flagged sub-10% flier: a +675 dog whom Brett notes has "better odds to win than he'll be owned," at the lowest ownership on the entire slate (5%), so the leverage is maximal if the unthinkable happens. The path is surviving Dulatov's early blitz and grinding out a decision. The reality is that his "most likely outcome is they get killed quickly" — chin issues, a 45% striking defense, and roughly a 12% win chance make this the thinnest path on the board. Framework-consistent Fade: a sub-10% dog under ~15% win equity is a dart, not leverage. — Fade
