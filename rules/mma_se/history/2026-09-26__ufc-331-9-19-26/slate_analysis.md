# UFC 331 — Van vs. Pantoja (9/19/26) — MMA slate strategy

_DraftKings only. One contest: the UFC $25K Mini Max, 150-entry max, 59,453 entries, you play 100. Sources: DailyFan's "UFC 331 Beatdown" (Brett, Technical Tim, Gordon Clark), DailyFan's "UFC 331 Top Plays / Slate Summary", and the DailyFan DK projection sheet. Every number below names its source._

## The short version

1. This is a twelve-fight card full of big favorites, played as a 100-entry stack in a giant field where first place pays most of the money.
2. Points come from fighters who win, and this card has five favorites priced to win fast by knockout, so the top scores will come from early finishes plus a few high-volume decisions.
3. The crowd is piling onto three fighters at almost the same rate (Joshua Van, Arman Tsarukyan, Iwo Baraniewski), and about one lineup in four will hold any two of them together.
4. The one decision that separates stacks this week is how much of the 100 rides that three-man chalk cluster versus the favorites with the same chance to win at a third of the crowd (Joanderson Brito, Dooho Choi, JooSang Yoo, Charles Jourdain).
5. A sharp stays chalky on purpose, because the top 1% of these big fields has been chalkier than the field, but picks WHICH favorites, and holds the knockout-or-nothing favorites at a rate instead of everywhere.
6. The biggest trap is the cheap underdog the crowd buys as a "safe" salary saver: Mauricio Ruffy, Brunno Ferreira, and Michael Aswell Jr. are owned like coin flips while the odds say they lose about two times in three.
7. The main event is a real coin flip, so the crowd's 50-to-42 split between Van and Pantoja is close to fair, and the sharp's edge there is small.
8. Nothing here is a lineup. Build in the Sim, and let the rules block at the bottom screen the set.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually matter this week. It states the data and stops. It does not tell you who to play.

1. **Three chalk anchors at one price.** Van 50%, Tsarukyan 48%, Baraniewski 48% (DailyFan own) sit within 2 points — the crowd treats them as one interchangeable cluster (the anchor-equivalence tension).
2. **Same win chance, a third of the crowd.** Brito is 77% to win at 17% own; Tsarukyan is 75% at 48% (DailyFan). The crowd pays nearly 3× for the same wager.
3. **The 64–71% tier is priced four ways.** Choi 71%/24%, Jourdain 65%/15%, Yoo 64%/12% versus O'Neill 66%/36% and Baraniewski 67%/48% (DailyFan) — same chance, ownership from 12% to 48%.
4. **The field pairs Van + Tsarukyan in ~24% of lineups, about 14,269 of 59,453**; Van + Baraniewski the same; Tsarukyan + Baraniewski ~23% (~13,674). All three together lands in roughly 1 lineup in 9.
5. **Cheap dogs owned like coin flips.** Ruffy 24% own on a 25% win chance, Ferreira 31% on 39%, Aswell 26% on 36% (DailyFan). Their ownership matches or beats their chance to win.
6. **Your opponents' habit, 2 of 2 past 150-Max fields:** ~8 names at ~34% own each, only 26.5% of rosters unique, the most-copied roster 177 times. The losing half kept buying the same price shape: 25%+-owned names in the $8–10K tier (8 of 16), 9 of 16 owned ahead of projection.
7. **"Owned ahead of projection" names this slate:** O'Neill (+14 ranks), Moura (+12), Aswell (+11), Ferreira (+10), Shahbazyan (+8), Baraniewski (+5). The validated 8/29 lesson says this screen is NOT a trap signal in MMA — three of six flagged fighters filled half of both 8/29 winning rosters — so read it as "the crowd is buying the win case," not "fade."
8. **Your process trend: bust exposure 60% four slates running.** Your entries keep carrying the card's biggest losers, and on 9/12 those were mid-priced favorites who lost (Silva, Rahiki, Garcia). The knockout-or-nothing favorites here — Baraniewski, Shahbazyan, Gandra, Despaigne — are the same shape.
9. **The four-chalk team does not exist under the cap.** Steveson + Tsarukyan + Van + Baraniewski cost $36,000 and leave $14,000 for two slots; only two sub-$7,200 punts fit, which breaks the one-punt rule. Something popular has to come out.
10. **Set diversity is a rate, not a lineup rule (100 entries).** The best 100+-entry stacks on 9/5 and 9/12 ran 2–4 fighters above 50% exposure, 23–28 fighters used, and summed ownership 165–188% per entry — a starting envelope, still a hypothesis after two cards.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows how the crowd will build, how a sharp will build, and where the two split.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry takes one main-event side (Van + Pantoja add to 92% own, so nearly every lineup has one), adds Tsarukyan and Baraniewski, then fills with a $8,400–8,500 favorite (O'Neill 36%, Shahbazyan 31%) and a cheap "floor" dog (Aswell 26%, Menifield 26%, Ruffy 24%, Ferreira 31%). Past 150-Max fields show only 26.5% of rosters were unique and the most-copied lineup appeared 177 times, so this build will exist hundreds of times.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The pro who has played your contests most (moklovin, seen 22 times, ahead of you 9 of 22) runs about 29.8% ownership per slot — that is chalky, about 179% summed per entry — carries a sub-5%-owned piece in about 27% of lineups, and holds the top anchor in about half. Applied here: the sharp anchors on Tsarukyan and a main-event side at near-field rates, sources the rest of the win chance from the under-owned favorites (Brito 17%, Choi 24%, Yoo 12%, Jourdain 15%), caps the knockout-or-nothing favorites at a rate, and takes the one live punt (Diaz 6%) in roughly 1 entry in 5.

**The gap.** The field pays 48% ownership for a 75% winner (Tsarukyan) and 36% for a 66% winner (O'Neill); the sharp buys the same win chances at 17% (Brito) and 12–15% (Yoo, Jourdain). Both build chalky. The sharp's chalk is just the cheaper copy of the same odds.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through a sharp's decisions in order, for a 100-entry stack in a 59,453-entry field where first place is most of the money. Suggestions here are in the sharp's voice. Players are named; no roster is.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors from the $9,100 slot: Tsarukyan (48%) has a decision floor and a finish path, so he can run hot across the stack. The main event is the second anchor slot, split near the odds — Van (50%) and Pantoja (42%) are one coin flip, never both in one lineup. The three-way cluster Van/Tsarukyan/Baraniewski is the anchor-equivalence twin set; a sharp treats Baraniewski as the swappable third, not a fixture.
2. **The leverage piece.** On this card the leverage is under-owned FAVORITES, not dogs: Brito (17%), Choi (24%), Yoo (12%), Jourdain (15%). The pro envelope (moklovin: a sub-5% piece in ~27% of lineups) puts Diaz (6%) in roughly 1 entry in 5 and Sharaf/Tuivasa in none.
3. **What the field duplicates.** Van + Tsarukyan lands in ~14,269 lineups; Van + Baraniewski the same; all three together in roughly 6,800. A pair count is information, not a rule. This card's own read argues against ONE pair: Baraniewski is knockout-or-nothing, so a sharp caps him near 60% of the stack rather than banning the pair — and when a pair is split, the less-owned half is the one kept (open 8/29 lesson).
4. **The salary shape.** Tsarukyan + Steveson cost $19,000 and leave $7,750 a slot for four fighters. Add Van and Baraniewski and only $14,000 remains for two slots, which only two sub-$7,200 punts can fill. So the real decision is which popular name comes out: Steveson lineups drop a mid-priced favorite and lean on Menifield/Pitbull/Ferreira; Baraniewski-free lineups buy Choi or Brito with the savings.
5. **Two contests, two different entries.** One contest is declared, so this step does not apply. Inside the 100, the set is judged as rates: 2–4 fighters above 50% exposure, 23–28 fighters used, every entry distinct.
6. **The big-field attack.** One line per crowd mistake with evidence on this card:
   - Self-copying · Van+Tsarukyan ~14,269 pairs, past fields 26.5% unique · the most-copied build splits first place hundreds of ways.
   - Mispriced ownership · Ruffy 24%/25% win, Ferreira 31%/39%, Aswell 26%/36% · the losing half buys these as floors.
   - Recency chasing · Menifield "scored 127 last time... will keep his ownership inflated," O'Neill "averaging 114 in wins... due for regression" · the crowd pays for last fight.
   - Anchor-twin herding · Tsarukyan 48% and Brito 17% at the same 75–77% win chance · the same odds are on sale.
   - Ignored mid-price tier · Yoo 12%, Jourdain 15%, Brito 17%, Despaigne 20% at $8,800–9,600 · four favorites the crowd skips to afford the cluster.
   - Dead builds · 3.4% of past entries; Sharaf (7% to win) and Tuivasa (19%) are this card's dead slots.
   - Trap-shaped prices · O'Neill 36% at $8,500 (+14 ranks ahead of projection) · in MMA this is the crowd buying the win case, so a sharp underweights, not fades.
   The effective field is smaller than 59,453.
7. **The pre-lock check** (yes/no, on each built lineup):
   - Does it hold only ONE of Van/Pantoja?
   - Can every one of the six win his fight in one sentence, including the cheapest slot?
   - Does it carry at most two $9,000+ fighters, and does each have a finish path?
   - Does it carry no more than one fighter under $7,200, and is that one Diaz rather than Sharaf/Tuivasa?
   - If it holds Baraniewski, is the stack still under 60% on him?

Target envelope in one line: moklovin's ~29.8% ownership per slot, a sub-5% piece in about 1 entry in 4, the top anchor in about half — chalky by design, different by which favorites.
