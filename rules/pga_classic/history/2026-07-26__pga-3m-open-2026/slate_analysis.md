## Edges & tensions

- **The slate is one giant Scheffler decision.** He's $14,900 with a 140-point ceiling and 25.2% projected ownership (ETR), and his price hard-locks any lineup with him into a 14/7/7/7/6/6 salary shape — so the real edge isn't him, it's whether the forced $6K–$7K pieces around him can crack a top-10 (the Large Field writer is "HEAVY OVERweight, potential FULL LOCK" precisely because this course's variance boosts the cheap tier).
- **Anchor-Equivalence sits in the $9K tier, not at the top:** **Maverick McNealy** (23.6%, 114.3 ceiling), **Kurt Kitayama** (23.3%, 117.6), and **Tom Kim** (22.4%, 110.4) are priced within $600 of each other at near-identical ownership — the field treats them as interchangeable, and the ceilings say Kitayama's is the tallest of the three (ETR).
- **Duplication tension — the chalk pairs:** the field will pair **Scheffler + McNealy** in ~5.9% of lineups (~112 of this 1,902-entry field), **Scheffler + Kitayama** ~5.9% (~112), **Scheffler + Tom Kim** ~5.6% (~107), and the $9K-on-$9K pairs (McNealy+Kitayama ~105, McNealy+Tom Kim ~101, Kitayama+Tom Kim ~99) each start the week shared with ~100 other entries. A lineup carrying any of these pairs begins the night inside that crowd.
- **Skill is compressed here — the fit model docks the best players.** ETR's Course Preview says approach/driving skill has NOT been predictive at TPC Twin Cities (penalty strokes make losing strokes far easier than gaining them), so its course-fit "docks" (projection trims) hit the longest, best ball-strikers — **Keith Mitchell** (dock #1), **Gary Woodland** (#2), **Jake Knapp** (#3), **Michael Brennan** (#4), even **Scheffler** (#8) — while boosts go to short/accurate profiles. Random winners (Hodges, Vegas, Champ) are this course's history.
- **The Grillo drift problem:** ETR projects **Emiliano Grillo** at just 9.3% with the #1 course-history boost, but ETG's Sam says "he could be over 20% ownership… I don't see how people don't play him." At 9% he's leverage; at 20% he's chalk — the single biggest lock-window ownership swing on the board.
- **Vendor-vs-panel splits are the leverage signal on a one-vendor week:** ETR's coffin list is cold on **Jake Knapp** (playing him 8.1 points LESS than the field will own him: 3.0% exposure vs 11.1% own) while ETG's Sky calls him "all systems go" post-injury with a 106.7 ceiling — and ETR is cold on **Tony Finau** (2.0% exposure vs 7.0% own) despite his 2022 win here. Note the coffin gaps are ETR disagreeing with ETR's own ownership projection (a self-referential signal — the validated self-erosion guard says size them as notes, not headline bets, unless a second independent reason exists).

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

**How the FIELD plays it.** ETG's Cody expects the field to "go more of the balanced direction" — the mass of lineups run 9/9/8/8/7/7 without touching the $6K tier, jamming the interchangeable $9K trio (McNealy 23.6% / Kitayama 23.3% / Tom Kim 22.4%) plus **Hideki Matsuyama** (19.8%, ownership that doubled off one good Open week per the Large Field writer). The Scheffler quarter of the field defaults into the same forced value cluster — **Ben Kohles** appeared in 28% of simulated Scheffler lineups (ETG Cody), with **Casey Jarvis** (13.8%), **Michael Kim**, **Max McGreevy**, and **Brian Harman** all in ~20% Scheffler-combo territory. The typical entry is Scheffler-or-a-$9K, Conners (16.2%) or Kohles (19.2%) as the comfort value, and zero exposure below $6,600.

**How a SHARP plays it.** Your observed golf shark envelope runs **16.4% ownership per slot, a sub-5% piece in 47% of lineups, 0.41 chalk-anchor exposure, 89% unique** — they take ONE anchor from the top (Scheffler or the highest-ceiling $9K), then differentiate everything downstream. On this board the sharp's sub-10% piece lives in the $7K definer band (Greyserman, Keefer, Michael Kim, Coody) and their sub-5% dart comes from the value-report/form tier (Yu, Jaeger, Lipsky, Ewart), not from random $6K punts. What they refuse to share: the default combos — the $9K double-stack and the Scheffler+Kohles auto-pair.

**The gap.** The field pays 22–25% ownership three times over for one substitutable $9K ceiling; the sharp buys that ceiling once and gets equivalent ceiling at 5–10% ownership in the $7K–$8K band (Coody 104.8 ceiling at 8.6%, Woodland 105.2 at 5.3%, Greyserman 101.7 at 7.7% — vs Tom Kim 110.4 at 22.4%). On a skill-compressed course, that ownership discount is nearly free ceiling.

## Key themes

- **This is a one-vendor slate, so every "edge" needs the self-reference check.** All ownership and projection numbers are ETR's; the coffin list (overweights Kohles +11.9, Harman +9.5, M.Kim +9.1, McNealy +9.0 / underweights Knapp -8.1, Mitchell -8.0, Suber -7.9) is ETR's optimal exposure minus ETR's own ownership projection. The validated lesson: those gaps compress toward zero when the vendor's own subscribers steam the name (watch Grillo 9.3% → Sam's "20%+", Kohles "ownership tied to Scottie"), so they're sizing notes, not conviction bets.
- **The sources' live disagreements are the slate's leverage map:** Hideki (Sky "maybe win" vs writer "no ceiling"), Sungjae (Sky flag plant vs writer off), Knapp (ETG "all systems go" vs coffin -8.1), Finau (2022 champ course history vs both ETR and Sky out). Each split is a place the field's consensus is thinner than the 20% ownership suggests.
- **Structure beats selection on a skill-compressed course.** ETR's own Course Preview concedes skill hasn't predicted outcomes here ("variance reigns supreme"), the panel says it's "tough a week to get 6/6" — which shifts the edge from picking the right chalk to holding cheap ceiling the field refuses (the $7K definer band above) while the field pays 22–25% three times over for interchangeable $9Ks.

## How to approach the slate

Four bullets in a 1,902-field, top-heavy 5-Max: every lineup a distinct thesis, and the win — not a min-cash — is the only outcome that matters. The birdie-fest-with-cut archetype calibrates to **~2 (max 2.5) sub-10% pieces per lineup at ~13% average per-player ownership** (168-slate mining baseline), and your observed golf shark envelope (16.4% own/slot, sub-5% piece in 47% of lineups, 0.41 anchor exposure, 89% unique) says: carry the elite anchor without fear, keep ONE sub-5% dart in about half the bullets — not one-plus-extras in all of them (your dart rate has run ABOVE the in-field pros two straight slates while the definers landed in the 8–19% band) — and spend the freed slots on that 8–19% band. moklovin (beat you 1 of 2, 33% own/slot, 20% leverage) rides the chalk anchors and skips the darts entirely — the sub-5% piece he never carries is exactly where your 4 bullets can beat him. Spread the Scheffler-yes/Scheffler-no split and the $9K-trio choice ACROSS the four bullets rather than repeating one answer; keep every dart from the definers screen above.
