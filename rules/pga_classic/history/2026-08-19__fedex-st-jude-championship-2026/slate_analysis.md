# FedEx St. Jude Championship — slate strategy (DK Classic, 8/13/26)

## The short version

1. This is the first playoff event: a tiny 69-man field, and every golfer plays all four rounds because there is no cut.
2. The course, TPC Southwind in Memphis, punishes wild drives with water and thick rough, so points come from accurate drivers who hit greens.
3. Because nobody can get cut, a player's best-case week (his ceiling) matters more than his safe average score.
4. The crowd will load up on Scottie Scheffler — likely 1 of every 3 entries by lock.
5. The crowd's next favorites (Justin Thomas, Cameron Young, Sam Burns) each land on roughly 1 in 4 to 5 entries.
6. The one decision that separates entries: pay $13,500 for Scheffler, or spend that money on two mid-priced stars the crowd half-ignores.
7. A sharp keeps the average pick-rate of his six golfers moderate and hides at least one rarely-picked, big-ceiling player in every entry.
8. The biggest trap is the mid-priced hot-name tier — players picked for last week's story harder than this week's numbers deserve.

## Build it like a sharp

The walk-through of a sharp's decisions on this slate, in order. Suggestions here are in sharp voice; every pick is yours.

1. **The anchor decision.** Three paths: Scheffler (27%, steaming toward 33–35%), McIlroy at 13.2%, or skipping the $11K+ tier for two $9.5K–$10.2K stars. A sharp treats Scheffler as chalk to size — the observed pros hold the crowd's anchor in about 2 of 5 lineups — and notes the Fleetwood/Schauffele twins are one bet where Fleetwood is the less-shared side.
2. **The leverage piece.** The pros carried a sub-5%-owned player in about half their lineups (your observed shark envelope: 49.5%). The sub-5% shelf with a story: Rose (4.3%), Spieth (4.6%), Noren (3.9%); one tier up, MacIntyre and Poston.
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Carrying Scheffler + Thomas means sharing that pair with ~46 of the 705 entries before a ball is struck; Scheffler + Young is ~44 more of the same.
4. **The salary shape.** Scheffler leaves $36,500 for five spots — a $7,300 average, exactly the tier the Large Field writer devalues; his fix is 13/8/8/6/6/6 (two $8Ks, three $6Ks). McIlroy leaves a $7,700 average, which fits the $8K-heavy 11/8/8/8/6/6 shape. If the $9K chalk can't all fit, WHICH of Young/Burns to drop is the real decision.
5. **Two entries, two theses.** With 2 bullets in one top-heavy 705-field contest, each entry is its own tournament: the classic split is one Scheffler-path entry and one non-Scheffler shape (never zero the #1 — codified).
