# NFL Showdown slate strategy — Giants at Rams, Monday 9/21/26

_DraftKings only. Sources: Evan Silva's Matchups (written by John Daigle), the ETR Showdown Breakdown (Cody Main), and the ETR projection sheet with captain-slot and flex-slot ownership. One contest: the $70K First Down, 20-Max, 83,234 entries, 20 of your entries._

## The short version

This section is the whole slate in plain words, for someone who knows nothing about DFS.

1. This is a one-game contest where the Rams are favored by about a touchdown, so most lineups will be built around the Rams winning comfortably.
2. Points come from five players: the two quarterbacks, the Rams' top receiver Davante Adams, the Rams' running back Kyren Williams, and the Giants' receiver Malik Nabers, because the Rams' star receiver Puka Nacua is expected to sit out and his catches have to go somewhere.
3. The crowd is putting all five of those stars in most lineups, and it is putting Adams in the captain spot (the one player who scores one-and-a-half times his points) more than anyone else.
4. The decision that separates entries is which star wears the captain tag and which star gets left out, because all five stars plus a captain do not fit under the salary cap.
5. A sharp player captains from the Rams' cluster but leans on the running back, because a favored team that leads runs the ball, and running-back captains beat their price more often than any other position.
6. A sharp also spreads twenty entries across different game stories instead of entering the same "Rams win big" lineup twenty times.
7. The biggest trap is the cheap Rams pass-catcher the whole crowd buys, Colby Parkinson, because when a star is out the field piles onto the same replacement and about one lineup in three will already own him.
8. The second trap is stacking three or four Giants pass-catchers in a "Giants throw late" lineup, because your last slate lost most of its points exactly that way.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the eight things that matter most on this slate. Each line is an observation, not an order.

1. **Nacua's missing 30% of targets is the slate.** Both articles say Adams inherits the most, but the Breakdown says "no single pass catcher" replaces him. The field pays Adams 62.4% ownership for a share that will spread across four cheap Rams pieces.
2. **Anchor-equivalence, the quarterbacks:** Dart (72.9% owned, 15.8% at captain) and Stafford (71.4%, 12.1% at captain) are priced $200 apart and project within one point. The field treats them as the same player.
3. **Anchor-equivalence, the Rams stars:** Adams (62.4%, 18.0% at captain) and Kyren Williams (57.5%, 15.4% at captain) sit within 5 points of ownership. They are two different bets on the same Rams drive, one through the air and one on the ground.
4. **The running-back captain is under-used relative to his edge.** Adams is the most-captained player at 18.0%. Williams is captained 15.4% with Silva's "20 routes to Corum's 2" and 67% of the carries inside the 5-yard line.
5. **ETR calls Corum and Likely "leverage," but its own sheet owns them 27.3% and 29.0%.** Their leverage lives only at the captain spot (Corum 3.5%, Likely 4.2%), not in the flex.
6. **Trap-shaped prices this slate:** Stafford (71.4% owned, 3 ranks ahead of his projection), Dart (1 rank ahead), Parkinson ($3,600, 32.8%, 1 rank ahead), Mevis ($5,000, 23.2%, 1 rank ahead). The crowd pays more ownership than the projection earns.
7. **The duplication magnet:** the field pairs Dart + Stafford in about 52% of lineups (~43,000 entries). The most-copied full shape is Adams at captain with Dart, Stafford and Williams at flex plus Parkinson and Ferguson, about 130 exact copies before the game starts.
8. **The field's sixth spot converges.** ETR projects Parkinson 32.8%, Ferguson 22.9% and Xavier Smith 11.0% at $200. The Breakdown's own advice is "avoid piling into ownership" among those cheap Rams pieces.
9. **Your last slate's bust pattern repeats the setup:** a 6.5-point spread where the underdog's pass-catchers busted while your set held them at double the field's rate. The Giants catchers (Nabers 51.6%, Likely 29.0%, Mooney 8.6%, Fields 11.6%) are this slate's version.
10. **The vendor and the article disagree on the ceiling order.** The Breakdown calls Williams "the slate's second-highest ceiling" and Dart the third. The sheet says Adams 31.2, Dart 29.0, Williams 28.6.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows the two pictures you build against: what most lineups look like, and what a sharp's lineup looks like.

**How the field plays it.** The typical entry carries four of the five stars (Dart, Stafford, Adams, Williams, Nabers) and fills the last two spots with Parkinson and Ferguson, the two cheap Rams pieces every projection sheet points to. ETR's own build-shape table says the field goes 4 Rams-2 Giants 38.3% of the time and 3-3 27.0%, so the mass of lineups is Rams-heavy with Adams or Dart at captain.

**How a sharp plays it.** The named pro in your logged contests, moklovin, runs about 30% ownership per roster spot and carries a sub-5% piece in about one lineup of four. Showdown winners in your logged slates ran chalkier, about 45–48% per spot, and differed from the field by the captain tag and the cheap sixth piece, not by fading stars. Applied here, a sharp keeps the stars, captains Williams or Nabers more often than Adams, and finds the sixth piece where the crowd is not: Mooney at 8.6%, Mumpfield at 9.7%, Singletary at 6.9%, or the Giants defense at 9.3%.

**The gap.** The field pays 18.0% captain ownership for Adams and 32.8% flex ownership for Parkinson. A sharp gets a similar Rams-scoring ceiling from Williams at captain (15.4%) and gets a real route-runner in the sixth spot at under 10% ownership.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes on this slate, in order. It names candidates; you pick. Twenty entries in one large contest means one lineup per game story, never one lineup repeated.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp captains from the Rams cluster and leans running back: Williams (15.4% at captain) first, Adams (18.0%) second, because the favorite that leads runs the ball and Williams owns the goal line. Nabers (11.8% at captain) is the sharp's Giants captain. The quarterbacks are anchor-equivalence twins (Dart 15.8%, Stafford 12.1% at captain) and both sit under the 20% captain rate that puts a quarterback on the captain menu, so Dart earns tags and Stafford earns a few. Adams and Williams are the second twin pair: a sharp holds both at flex but does not captain Adams in more than 3 of 20 entries.
2. **The leverage piece.** The sharp's low-owned piece comes from the sixth spot or the captain tag, not from dropping a star. Candidates: Mooney (8.6%), Singletary (6.9%), Mumpfield (9.7%), Giants defense (9.3%), and at captain Likely (4.2%) or Parkinson (3.5%). Target moklovin's rate: a sub-5% piece in about 5 of 20 entries, not in every one.
3. **What the field duplicates.** Dart + Stafford appear together in about 52% of lineups (~43,000 entries). The most-copied full shape is Adams at captain with Dart, Stafford, Williams, Parkinson and Ferguson at $48,000, about 130 exact copies before kickoff. Carrying Dart and Stafford both at flex is fine and a sharp does it often; the pair that a sharp treats as one bet is Adams-at-captain plus Williams-at-flex plus Parkinson, because that is the exact copy shape.
4. **The salary shape.** Adams at captain costs $13,200, leaving $36,800 for five spots, or $7,360 each. Dart, Stafford and Williams take $29,400 of that, leaving $7,400 for two spots, so Nabers ($9,000) cannot fit. The real decision is which star to drop: the field drops Nabers, so a sharp's Nabers-in lineup drops Stafford instead. Williams at captain ($14,400) leaves $35,600 and the same choice. Parkinson at captain ($5,400) is the only way to carry four stars at flex ($38,400) with $6,200 left.
5. **Twenty entries, five stories.** One contest, so the split is by story. Rams blowout (5-1) about 4 entries, Rams control the game (4-2, Williams or Corum plus the Rams defense) about 5, back-and-forth game (3-3 with Dart and a Rams catcher) about 6, Rams lead and the Giants throw late (2-4 tilted to the Giants, captain from the Rams side) about 3, Giants upset (Skattebo or Dart captain with the Giants defense) about 2. Your last slate's lesson caps the Giants-catcher stories at about 5 entries combined, and no lineup holds more than 3 Giants pass-catchers.
6. **The big-field attack.** The mistakes with evidence on this slate:
   - Self-copying · Adams-captain chalk shape ~130 copies · the Williams and Nabers captain tags.
   - Mispriced ownership · Stafford 71.4% at 3 ranks ahead of projection · Stafford in at most 12 of 20.
   - Anchor-twin herding · Dart 72.9% / Stafford 71.4% · the entry that holds one quarterback with a catcher stack.
   - Ignored mid-price tier · Mooney $3,800 at 8.6% versus Parkinson $3,600 at 32.8% · the route-runner sixth piece.
   - News stampede · Nacua out, so Parkinson 32.8%, Ferguson 22.9%, Smith 11.0% · Mumpfield and Atwell at the same price with a third of the ownership.
   - Dead builds · Ricard, Rivers, Manhertz, Klare, Berrios at 2.4 ceiling or less · zero of them in any entry.
   - Trap-shaped prices · Parkinson and Mevis owned 1 rank ahead · both capped across the set.
   The effective field is smaller than 83,234: the half that shares the Dart + Stafford + Adams core is competing for one slice.
7. **The pre-lock check.** Ask each lineup:
   - Can it say in one sentence which team wins and how (its story name)?
   - If Adams is captain with Dart, Stafford and Williams at flex, is sharing about 130 copies accepted on purpose?
   - Does a Giants-throw-late lineup hold more than 3 Giants pass-catchers?
   - Does it hold Fields or Johnson when Parkinson, Ferguson or Mooney projects higher for less?
   - Does it have more than 2 kicker-plus-defense pieces, or a kicker next to its own captain quarterback?

Sharp-envelope target in one line: about 45% ownership per roster spot across the set, a sub-5% piece in roughly 5 of 20 entries, every entry unique, the way moklovin's logged entries hold the chalk and differ by the captain tag and the sixth piece.
