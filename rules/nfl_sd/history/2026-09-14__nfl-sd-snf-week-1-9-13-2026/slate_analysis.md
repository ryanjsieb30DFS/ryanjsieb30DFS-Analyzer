# NFL Showdown — Cowboys at Giants (Sunday Night, 9/13/26)

## The short version

1. This is a one-game contest where you pick six players from Cowboys–Giants, one of them a Captain who scores 1.5 times his points.
2. Vegas expects a close, medium-scoring game, so the points come from a few high-volume players on both teams rather than one team running away.
3. The crowd is stacking both quarterbacks together, with Dak Prescott and Jaxson Dart each landing on about two of every three teams.
4. The one decision that separates entries tonight is the Captain, because the crowd spreads its Captain vote almost evenly across five stars and leaves Cam Skattebo and Malik Nabers nearly untouched there.
5. A sharp bets on one story of how the game goes, picks the Captain that story needs, and leaves a little salary unspent so nobody else lands on the same six players.
6. The biggest trap is paying for the five most popular players at once, because they cannot all fit under the salary cap and the leftovers are the same cheap fillers everyone else buys.
7. The second trap is the Dallas kicker, who costs more and is picked far more often than the Giants kicker for almost the same points.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually matter tonight, with the number or article line that shows each one.

1. **Nobody is over-captained.** The field's Captain vote is Lamb 14.4%, Dak 13.8%, Williams 13.8%, Dart 13.3%, Pickens 11.8% (ETR) — five near-equal choices, none above the 1-in-5 rate a quarterback actually wins.
2. **The under-captained ceilings are Skattebo and Nabers.** Skattebo is Captain on 7.6% of teams with a best night (ceiling) of 25.6; Nabers 6.4% with 25.3 (ETR).
3. **The chalk cannot fit.** Dak, Dart, Williams, Lamb, and Pickens cost $49,600 at flex alone, so every team drops at least one — which one is the real decision.
4. **Anchor-Equivalence:** Lamb ($10,800, 52.1%) and Pickens ($9,800, 47.7%) are near-twins; ETR calls them "nearly interchangeable," negatively correlated (−0.33) last season.
5. **Silva and Main disagree on Skattebo.** Silva: "volatile RB3" coming off an open fracture; Main: "no-doubt RB1," underpriced, overlooked. That gap is the edge.
6. **Article vs sheet on Nabers.** Main expects "reasonably low" ownership; the ETR sheet has him at 34.2% total — 1 of every 3 teams.
7. **Duplication:** the field pairs Dak + Dart in about 42% of lineups, roughly 823 of 1,960; last week's most-copied Showdown lineup appeared 88 times.
8. **Trap-shaped prices (owned ahead of projection):** Dart, Dak, Aubrey, Tracy, Flournoy, Giants DST — six prices where the crowd's pick rate ranks higher than the projection ranks.
9. **Your past opponents' shape (2 comparable SE contests):** they pile onto ~8 names per contest and only 59.1% of rosters were unique; the top-3 chalk rode together in 36.6% of lineups. Trap shape: 6 of 16 traps were owned ahead of projection, most in the under-$6K tier.
10. **Under-built shapes:** ETR's construction table has 1 Cowboy–5 Giants at 2.4% and 2–4 at 17.9%, while 3–3 and 4–2 take two-thirds of the field.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows how the crowd will build and how a sharp builds differently.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry holds both quarterbacks at flex (Dak 52.7% flex own, Dart 49.8%), adds Javonte Williams and one of Lamb or Pickens, and fills the last two spots with the cheap pieces that make the math work: Theo Johnson (13.0%), Ryan Flournoy (17.1%), Tyrone Tracy Jr. (16.6%), or Brandon Aubrey (30.1%). It builds 3–3 or 4–2 (66.5% of the field per ETR's table) and wears the Captain on a Cowboys star, with Dak, Williams, and Lamb each near 14%.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The sharp sits near the same ownership per slot as the crowd — the observed pro envelope is about 28–30% per slot (framework ~28%; moklovin 29.8 in your contests) — but picks a story first: a Giants-side Captain the crowd skips (Skattebo 7.6%, Nabers 6.4%), or a Dallas Captain with a Giants kicker or defense the crowd underuses. The sharp carries a sub-5% piece in roughly 1 of 4 entries, never in every one, and leaves $500–$1,500 on the table.

**The gap.** The field pays 13–14% Captain ownership for five interchangeable stars and then copies itself in the cheap tier; the sharp gets a 25-point ceiling at 6–8% Captain ownership on the Giants side, or the same Dallas Captain with a different, cheaper sixth piece.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes, in order, for tonight's game. Suggestions here are in the sharp's voice; the user picks.

1. **The Captain decision.** A sharp captains from the story, not the ownership, and tonight the story has two live sides. Dallas-controls candidates: Williams (13.8% CPT) or one of Lamb (14.4%)/Pickens (11.8%) — twins, one per lineup. Giants-side candidates: Dart (13.3%), Skattebo (7.6%), Nabers (6.4%). Both QBs sit under the 20% base rate, so Dak (13.8% CPT, 29.6 CPT projection) stays on the menu too.
2. **The leverage piece.** The sharp's low-owned piece is usually the Captain choice itself (Skattebo, Nabers) rather than a punt. When it is a flex piece, the candidates are Zvada (22.1% vs Aubrey's 30.1%), Turpin (8.3%), or Demercado (8.3%). Target: a sub-5% piece in about 1 of 4 entries — the pros' rate, never a per-lineup rule.
3. **What the field duplicates.** Dak + Dart together sit in roughly 823 of 1,960 lineups; add Williams and the top-3 chalk rode together in 36.6% of last week's lineups. That count is information, not a rule — double-QB is a strong shape. The one pair a sharp refuses is Lamb + Pickens in the same lineup (either slot): ETR measured them at −0.33 correlation with direct target competition, and the Anchor-Equivalence read says they are one bet. Step 1 and the Top plays rows say the same thing.
4. **The salary shape.** Captain Williams costs $13,500 and leaves $7,300 per remaining slot; Captain Lamb costs $16,200 and leaves $6,760. Williams-Captain plus Dak, Dart, and Lamb is $44,300, leaving $5,700 for two spots — so the last two are Johnson plus Flournoy/Tracy/Turpin, the copied tier. The real decision is which star to drop (usually one QB or one of the twins). A sharp leaves at least $500 unspent (the median winner left $1,400; only 7% spent all $50,000) and never carries three min-priced punts.
5. **Two contests, two different entries.** The fields are near-identical (1,960 and 1,902, both top-heavy), so the split is by story, not size. One entry is the Dallas-controls story (Williams or a twin as Captain, Cowboys DST or Aubrey, one Giants bring-back like Likely); it wins when Silva's 24–14 comes true. The other is the Giants-live story (Dart, Skattebo, or Nabers as Captain, Zvada or Giants DST, a Dallas bring-back); it wins in the 2–4 or 1–5 shape only 20% of the field builds.
6. **The big-field attack.** Not declared — both contests are single-entry.
7. **The pre-lock check.**
   - Does the lineup name one story (Dallas controls, shootout, slog, Giants upset), and does the Captain belong to that story?
   - Does it hold only ONE of Lamb and Pickens?
   - If it holds a QB as Captain, is there a bring-back from the other team?
   - Is any UNDERWEIGHT player (Aubrey, Flournoy, Tracy) in BOTH entries? If so, that is over the crowd, not under.
   - Is at least $500 unspent, and are the K + DST pieces two or fewer?

Target: about 28–30% ownership per slot, a sub-5% piece in roughly 1 of 4 entries, every entry unique — the envelope moklovin runs in your contests (29.8% per slot, leverage in 26%).
