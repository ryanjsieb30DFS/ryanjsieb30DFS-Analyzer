# NFL Showdown — 49ers at Rams (Melbourne), Thursday 9/10/26

## The short version

This is the whole slate in nine plain sentences.

1. This is a one-game contest: the 49ers and Rams play in Australia, and you pick six players from that one game.
2. The points come from two star players, Puka Nacua and Christian McCaffrey, because each gets about one of every five throws on his team.
3. The crowd knows this, so about two of every three teams will have Nacua, and the same for McCaffrey.
4. The one decision that separates entries is who wears the captain spot (the player who scores 1.5 times his points), because nearly half the crowd captains Nacua or McCaffrey.
5. A sharp keeps the stars but captains a different player from the same group of popular players, most often a receiver the crowd only captains 1 time in 20.
6. Vegas says the Rams win by a field goal in a game with a normal amount of scoring, so this is not a blowout slate and both teams belong in every lineup.
7. The biggest trap is the price, not a player: the crowd is buying the cheap Rams fillers (their tight ends, their defense, Blake Corum) at a rate well above what those players are expected to score.
8. The exact-cap chalk lineup (the two stars, both quarterbacks, a cheap tight end, and a $200 filler) spends every dollar and will be copied many times.
9. Winners usually leave about $1,400 unspent and carry one cheap player the crowd skips, so leaving money on the table is a feature here.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the facts that actually decide the slate, with the number behind each one.

1. **Game script weights.** Total 48.5 sits at the top of the "middle" range and the spread is only 3.5, so roughly: back-and-forth shootout 35%, normal competitive game 40%, low-scoring grind 15%, one-sided rout 10% (winner data: a rout needs a 7+ spread).
2. **The crowd over-captains the two stars, not the quarterbacks.** ETR has Nacua at 23.8% captain and McCaffrey at 20.5% captain (44% of all captains together), while Stafford is 7.7% and Purdy 6.4%; the typical winning captain is owned about 11%.
3. **Anchor-equivalence: Nacua and McCaffrey are ownership twins.** ETR: 65.5% vs 65.0% owned, 22.2 vs 21.3 projected, ceilings 37.0 vs 35.5; the field treats them as the same pick, but they play for opposite teams, so the captain choice sets which side the lineup leans.
4. **The duplication pair.** The field pairs Nacua + McCaffrey in about 42.6% of lineups, roughly 1,013 of the 2,378 in the Huddle; McCaffrey + Stafford is next at about 911 lineups.
5. **The writers disagree with Vegas on who scores.** Daigle expects new 49ers coordinator Raheem Morris to force Stafford underneath (5.8 yards per attempt in their Week 17 meeting), while the Breakdown expects the Rams to pass 64% of the time as they did last year (0.44 points-added per dropback vs SF).
6. **The 49ers' receivers are where a winning score hides at almost no captain ownership.** ETR captain own: Evans 4.9%, Kittle 2.3%, Deebo 2.0%, Stribling 1.8%; the Breakdown says "it's impossible to pinpoint which of the 49ers' pass catchers can find a slate-winning score."
7. **Trap-shaped prices on the Rams side.** The crowd owns the Rams defense 24.5% (13th in projection, 6.8 points), Ferguson 24.3% (6.5 projected), Parkinson 20.9% (5.8), Corum 29.5% (9.0): all owned several ranks ahead of what they are expected to score.
8. **The Rams backfield split is a live argument.** ESPN's Graziano reports a "pretty close to 50-50" carry split (Breakdown), while Daigle expects McVay to lean on Williams if the game stays close; Williams 37.2% owned vs Corum 29.5%.
9. **Both kickers are chalk.** Mevis 21.1% and Pineiro 21.3% owned, projected 8.7 and 8.4; a kicker sits in 40% of winning lineups, but more than 2 kicker-plus-defense pieces almost never wins.
10. **The exact-cap chalk build exists.** Nacua captain ($16,800) + McCaffrey + Stafford + Purdy + Ferguson + a $200 receiver totals exactly $50,000; only 7% of winners spend the full cap and the median winner leaves $1,400.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows how the crowd will build and how a sharp builds differently.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry captains Nacua or McCaffrey, carries the other one at flex, adds Stafford (51.2% flex own), and then fills the last three spots from the cheap Rams pieces: Corum, Ferguson, Parkinson, the Rams defense, or a kicker. That build leans 4-2 toward the Rams and spends nearly every dollar, so thousands of entries end up two or three players apart.

**How a SHARP will play it.** There is no logged Showdown envelope for this repo yet, so the target comes from the cross-vendor winner data: about 28% ownership per slot on average, one or two real leverage pieces, never an all-contrarian card. Applied here, a sharp holds ONE of the two stars at flex (never both, see below), puts the captain on a receiver from the popular group (Adams 8.4% captain, Evans 4.9%, Williams 8.2%), takes his low-owned piece from the 49ers' pass-catchers instead of the Rams' fillers, and leaves $500-$2,000 unspent.

**The gap.** The field pays 44% combined captain ownership to captain the two stars; a sharp gets a similar ceiling from Adams (28.0 ceiling) or Evans (24.0) at a captain slot the crowd fills 5% to 8% of the time. The field's salary savings come from the Rams' tight ends at 20% to 24% owned; a sharp's savings come from a 49ers piece at 5% owned or from simply not spending the money.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes on this slate, in order. Two single-entry contests means two lineups, and each one should tell a different game story.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors on ONE of the two stars per lineup (Nacua in the Rams-side entry, McCaffrey in the 49ers-side entry) and spends the captain on someone else: Adams (8.4% captain) in the Rams-win story, Evans (4.9%) or McCaffrey (20.5%) in the 49ers-win story, Williams (8.2%) in the grind. Nacua and McCaffrey are ownership twins (65.5% vs 65.0% owned), so one twin per lineup is the anchor-equivalence read: each entry holds one and the other twin is the swap. The crowd already fills 44% of captain slots with the pair.

2. **The leverage piece.** A sharp's low-owned player is a 49ers pass-catcher, not a Rams filler: Deebo (17.5%), Stribling (18.2%), Kittle (19.5%), or Tonges (4.8%). The captain slot itself is the biggest leverage on this slate, since Evans at 4.9% captain owns a 24.0 ceiling.

3. **What a sharp refuses.** Carrying Nacua + McCaffrey at flex together means sharing that core with about 1,013 of the 2,378 Huddle entries before kickoff. A sharp also refuses the exact-$50,000 build and refuses more than two combined kicker-plus-defense pieces.

4. **The salary shape.** A Nacua captain takes $16,800 and leaves $6,640 per remaining slot; McCaffrey captain takes $15,900 and leaves $6,820. Both stars plus Stafford costs $37,200, leaving $4,267 per spot for three players, which is exactly why the crowd lands on Corum, Ferguson, and Parkinson. The real decision is which quarterback to drop: both QBs plus both stars fits only with the $200 fillers, so a sharp keeps one QB and buys a real receiver with the difference.

5. **Two contests, two different entries.** The Dimeback (1,960 entries) gets the Rams-pass story: a Rams receiver captain, Stafford at flex, and a 49ers bring-back (89% of passing-script winners carried the other team). The Huddle (2,378 entries, the bigger and more copied field) gets the 49ers story: a 49ers captain from the low-owned group, Purdy with two of his catchers, and the 2-4 split leaning away from the captain's team, the most under-used winning shape. Both are competitive-game stories because the 3.5 spread does not support a rout.

6. **The big-field attack.** No 20-Max or 150-Max contest is declared, so this step is skipped.

7. **The pre-lock check.**
   - Does the lineup tell ONE game story (shootout, competitive, or grind), and does the captain fit that story?
   - If the captain is a quarterback, are 2 to 3 of his own catchers in the lineup; if a receiver, is his quarterback there with at most one more teammate?
   - Does a passing lineup carry a bring-back from the other team?
   - Does it avoid carrying both Nacua and McCaffrey at flex, or if it does, is there a written reason?
   - Is it under $50,000 on purpose, with no more than two kicker-plus-defense pieces?

Target envelope in one line: about 28% ownership per slot with one or two real leverage pieces, the shape of the cross-vendor Showdown winners; the closest coaching name on file is youdacao, the sharp with the best NFL top-1% rate (3.4%) in the playbook, whose lineups run moderate ownership with a low-owned piece in most but never all of them.
