Next slate: compare each quarterback's captain ownership to that 1-in-5 base rate, and when it sits well under, name him on the captain menu.

**The writers' disagreement was surfaced but never turned into a tier (grade: C).** Edge 5 said the Matchups writer picked the 49ers 21-17 against Vegas and expected Stafford to be forced into short throws. The pick rationale defended him twice with "this is a single entry so he is not in every bullet," which was true inside each contest and false across the slate. The strategy named 12 low-owned players who could decide the slate, and your entries carried none; Robinson was in both winning lineups and Black in one. You held all three chalk anchors that mattered (McCaffrey, Stafford); the pros held Stafford in half their entries. This is the first NFL Showdown slate, so no axis is recurring yet. **Why the Dimeback pick broke: it trusted a sim decimal over two strategy rules.** The pick took the single highest chance-to-win row (5.33%, about 1 in 19) and wrote that it trusted the sim over the strategy on the exact $50,000 spend, then built a 5-1 Rams lineup that failed the strategy's own receiver-captain check (Adams captain plus three more Rams catchers). The Huddle pick also overrode the strategy's 2-4 shape for a 3-3. Both overrides lost, and the framework's shape data was on the winning side both times. Next slate: when the rationale writes "I trusted the sim over the strategy," the number it trusts must be outside the noise band, or the strategy rule wins.
