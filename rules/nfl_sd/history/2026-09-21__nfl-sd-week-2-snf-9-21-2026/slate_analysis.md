# NFL Showdown slate strategy — Colts at Chiefs, Sunday Night, September 20, 2026

_DraftKings only. Written from both Establish The Run articles (Evan Silva's Matchups by John Daigle, and the Showdown Breakdown by Cody Main) plus the ETR NFL Showdown projection sheet. Every price and ownership number below is DraftKings. Where an article's printed price differs from the sheet (the Breakdown lists Walker at $10,400 and Kelce at $6,600), the sheet's price is used._

## The short version

This section is the whole slate in plain words, for someone who has never played this format.

1. This is a one-game slate, Colts at Chiefs, where you pick six players and one of them (the captain) scores extra but costs extra.
2. The betting line expects the Chiefs to win by about a touchdown, so most points should come from the Chiefs running the ball and Indianapolis throwing when it falls behind.
3. The crowd is piling onto the same three stars, Mahomes, Walker and Taylor, and putting Walker in the captain spot more than anyone else.
4. The one decision that separates entries is who wears the captain tag, because the two running backs take most of the field's captain spots while Mahomes and his pass-catchers are captained far less than their best nights deserve.
5. A sharp keeps the stars but changes the captain, ties each entry to one story of how the game goes, and leaves a little money unspent so the lineup is not a copy.
6. The biggest trap is the cheap tier the three stars force everyone into, where the two kickers and Kelce are each picked by about a quarter of the field for ordinary points.
7. With twenty entries in a huge field, the job is twenty different stories, not one best lineup entered twenty times.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually matter this week, with the numbers that show them. It states each one and stops.

1. **Script weights.** Total 46.5 is the middle band (no captain position dominates), but a 6.5 spread and Silva's 27–14 make the Chiefs-rout story (blowout 5-1) real; rough weights: Chiefs control it 35%, competitive game 35%, Colts throw late (garbage-time 2-4) 20%, Colts upset 10%.
2. **Mahomes is under-captained.** The sheet has him in 13.3% of captain spots against 69.3% of all lineups; a quarterback is the best captain about 1 game in 5, so 13.3% is below the base rate with the sheet's third-best ceiling (30.4).
3. **The running backs eat the captain spot.** Walker 20.1% and Taylor 14.0% of captain spots, 34% together, while Rice (7.5%) and Worthy (7.8%) captain under 8% each with best nights of 26.8 and 23.3.
4. **Anchor-Equivalence.** Walker ($10,600, 63.5% owned) and Taylor ($11,000, 55.8%) are the same bet: workhorse backs with every goal-line carry, priced $400 apart; Taylor is the less-picked twin and the Breakdown calls him a "tournament priority."
5. **The writers disagree on Taylor and Rice.** Silva says Taylor's one-game ceiling is "debatable" (two 90-yard rushers allowed by KC since 2024); the Breakdown calls him slate-winning. Silva makes Rice a "high-floor WR1"; the Breakdown says the run-heavy plan "concerns me."
6. **Downs is not the leverage the article says.** The Breakdown lists him as a leverage play; the sheet has him 30.9% owned, the ninth-most-picked player, so his low-ownership case is gone.
7. **Duplication.** The field pairs Mahomes + Walker in about 44% of lineups, roughly 20,900 of 47,562; adding Taylor at flex too is the near-max build with three $4,400 fillers left.
8. **Trap-shaped prices.** Butker (27.4%), Shrader (23.8%), Kelce (37.5%) and Mahomes (69.3%) are picked two ranks ahead of where their projections rank; Worthy (39.2%) and Jones (42.3%) one rank ahead.
9. **The cheap tier is one-sided.** With the stars at flex, the field's fillers are Chiefs pieces (Butker, Chiefs defense, Kelce, Worthy at 22–39%), while Pierce ($7,600) sits at 18.7% flex and 4.5% captain with a 20.3 ceiling.
10. **Your repeated leak is bust exposure.** Last three slates: 75%, 40%, 80% of your entries held a top underperformer, and on the 9/15 rout the busts were the losing side's passing game (Nix, Sutton, Waddle); this week the losing side is Indianapolis.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows how the crowd builds and how a sharp builds, so the user can see the gap.

**How the FIELD plays it.** The typical entry captains Walker or Taylor, carries Mahomes and the other back at flex, and fills the last three spots from the $4,200–$6,200 Chiefs tier: Kelce, Worthy, Butker, the Chiefs defense. The Breakdown's own table says 34.2% of lineups go 4 Chiefs–2 Colts and 31.5% go 3–3, and the Mahomes + Walker pair sits in about 44% of the field.

**How a SHARP plays it.** The pros in your logs run about 30% ownership per spot (moklovin, 29.8%) and a sub-5% piece in about 1 of 4 lineups (27%), but the 9/15 top 1% of this exact 47,562-entry contest averaged 40.9% per spot with zero sub-5% pieces. So a sharp keeps three or four stars, differentiates with the captain (Mahomes, Rice, Warren or Pierce instead of Walker), leaves $500–$1,500 unspent, and builds the 2 Chiefs–4 Colts garbage-time shape the field builds only 16.7% of the time.

**The gap.** The field spends 20.1% of its captain spots on Walker and 13.3% on Mahomes for nearly the same best night (35.9 vs 30.4); the sharp gets the Chiefs' scoring at 13.3% captain ownership through Mahomes, or at 7.5% through Rice, and refuses the three-star-plus-three-fillers copy.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes for this slate, in order. It names candidates; the user picks, and the Sim builds.

1. **The anchor decision.** The anchor tier is the $10,000–$11,000 stars, and the sharp separates at the captain slot, not by fading them. Captain candidates: Mahomes (13.3% captain, under the base rate), Rice (7.5%), Warren (5.9%), with Walker (20.1%) and Taylor (14.0%) as the twin pair; the twin call needs a lock-time ownership look, because both articles push Walker and the less-picked twin has flipped before (9/15, Nix over Mahomes).
2. **The leverage piece.** It lives at the captain slot and in the Colts' mid tier: Pierce (4.5% captain), Warren (5.9% captain), Chiefs DST (2.1% captain), Thornton (14.5% flex). Target: the 9/15 top 1% of this contest averaged 40.9% per spot with no sub-5% pieces, so one true low-picked piece in roughly a quarter of the twenty entries is the observed rate, never a per-lineup rule.
3. **What the field duplicates.** Mahomes + Walker together sits in about 20,900 lineups before kickoff; Mahomes + Taylor about 18,400. Both Chiefs pieces pay under one story (Chiefs score a lot), so the pair itself is not refused. What a sharp refuses is all three stars at flex under a cheap captain: that is the Breakdown's first stack idea, printed for every subscriber, and the near-max projection the copy machine lands on. Walker and Taylor are one bet (step 1), so an entry holding both at flex is paying $21,600 for one idea.
4. **The salary shape.** Captain Walker costs $15,900 and leaves $34,100 for five spots, $6,820 each. Add Mahomes and Taylor and $13,100 is left for three, $4,367 each: exactly the Butker, Shrader, Chiefs defense, Thornton tier. Captain Mahomes at $15,000 with Walker and Rice leaves $15,000 for three, $5,000 each: Worthy, Kelce, Butker to the dollar, and that is the copied Chiefs stack. The real decision is Taylor versus a Colts catcher at $7,200–$7,600 (Warren, Pierce) in the third big slot. Leaving $500–$1,500 is normal for winners (median $1,400 unspent, only 7% spent it all), but it is a rate, not a gate.
5. **Two contests, two different entries.** One contest is declared, so this is twenty entries, one story each: about 7 Chiefs-rout entries (5-1 or 4-2, Walker or Chiefs-defense anchored), 7 competitive entries (Mahomes or Rice captain, two Chiefs catchers, a Colts bring-back), 4 garbage-time entries (2 Chiefs–4 Colts, Downs/Pierce/Warren carrying Indy's late throws, Walker or Chiefs DST as the early anchor), 2 upset entries (Taylor or Jones captain, Colts defense, one Chiefs bring-back).
6. **The big-field attack.** Twenty-Max declared, so these are the field's mistakes with this slate's evidence.
   - Self-copying: 44% hold Mahomes + Walker, and the Breakdown printed its stack ideas; a different captain on the same stars is uncopied.
   - Mispriced ownership: Worthy 39.2% and Kelce 37.5% for the seventh and eighth projections; the same points at 18.7% through Pierce.
   - Anchor-twin herding: Walker 20.1% captain, Taylor 14.0%; the Mahomes captain at 13.3% sits below both for a bigger ceiling than Taylor's.
   - Ignored mid tier: Pierce ($7,600, 18.7% flex) and Warren at captain (5.9%) versus Allen at 26.0%.
   - Dead builds: eight players under $1,700 with ceilings of 1.3 or less; three of them in one entry is a dead lineup.
   - Trap-shaped prices: both kickers at 24–27% for 8–9 points outdoors.
   - Under-built shapes: 2 Chiefs–4 Colts is 16.7% of the field and 1–5 is 2.2%, so the losing-side stories are cheap to own.
   The effective field is far smaller than 47,562 once the copies are counted.
7. **The pre-lock check.** Ask each entry:
   - Does it name one story (rout, competitive, garbage-time, upset), and does the team split match that story?
   - If Mahomes is captain, does it carry two of Rice, Worthy, Kelce and one Colts pass-catcher?
   - Does it hold Mahomes, Walker and Taylor all at flex under a cheap captain, and if so, what makes it not the copy?
   - Does it carry a Colts pass-catcher without a story where Indianapolis throws?
   - Is it under three kicker-plus-defense pieces and free of both kickers?

Target envelope: about 40% ownership per spot across the set with the captain doing the differentiating, the way moklovin (29.8% per spot, a low-picked piece in about 1 of 4 lineups, 9 of 22 finishes ahead of yours) builds volume as breadth of stories, not one lineup repeated.
