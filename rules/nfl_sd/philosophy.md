# NFL Showdown Philosophy — Ryan's GPP Approach

**Seeded 2026-09-09 from docs/nfl_game_theory.md (Part 2). Updated 2026-09-10 with the ETR research digest — user-approved.**

## Core identity

GPP only, playing to WIN, never to cash. In Showdown that means: the captain call and the game script ARE the lineup — everything else is filling in the story. The user's Showdown profile is **5–20 entries in large-field lotto contests**, which is a different game from the SE/3-Max home game: duplication and portfolio coverage matter as much as raw projection.

## The five beliefs this format runs on

1. **The captain decision is the whole game.** The 1.5x multiplier is price-neutral, so captaining is purely a ceiling + field-behavior call. The field over-captains the QB roughly 5x his real win rate; his own pass-catchers out-captain him because the catcher out-scores the thrower on every completion. The default leverage move in this format is not an obscure player — it is the RIGHT member of the chalk cluster wearing the C. The mirror-image leak is the sub-5% captain chosen for ownership alone; winning captains live in the 5–25% band, and low ownership is a tiebreaker, never the thesis.
2. **Every lineup is one game story.** Shootout, slog, blowout-onslaught, garbage-time comeback — a lineup that mixes stories (a slog kicker next to a shootout double-stack) is a portfolio filler, not a bet. One entry per script, weighted by the Vegas total and spread. A 3-3 build with no script behind it is the default of players who lose money; if the lineup cannot say which side wins and how, it is not finished. Six skill players with no kicker and no defense is the same tell — a default, not a decision.
3. **Correlation is bought, not hoped for.** You don't know which receiver catches the TDs, so a pocket-QB captain buys 2–3 of them; a shootout pays both sides, so the bring-back is near-mandatory (89% of passing-build winners).
4. **Duplication is the silent prize-splitter.** The product of the six ownerships predicts copies; exact-$50K chalk is the most-copied shape. Winners leave salary (median $1,400 unspent). In large fields, the uniqueness levers (salary left, a kicker, the 2-4 split, one sub-3% piece) are cheap equity.
5. **Chalkier-than-Classic is normal here.** Winners average ~28% own per slot. Moderate chalk plus one or two real leverage decisions beats manufactured weirdness. Rates describe winners after the fact — no rate is ever a per-lineup quota.

## What never changes (house rules, all sports)

- The Analyzer synthesizes and names individual plays in tiers; it NEVER builds lineups.
- A trap is a price, not a player. Every fade cites THIS slate's numbers.
- Only a call this slate's strategy makes can cost a grade; every statistic is information.
- Best-percentile trend + process metrics are the scoreboard; ROI lives in the third-party app.
