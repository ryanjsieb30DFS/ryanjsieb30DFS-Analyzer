## Build it like a sharp

This closing section walks the decisions in the order a consistently winning player makes them, applied to this slate. Suggestions here are voiced as what the sharp does — you pick the players.

1. **The anchor decision.** A sharp anchors from the top of the leaderboard on a birdie-fest — the winner is in the winning DK lineup about 72% of the time on ETR's chart. The candidates: Kim ($10,400, 41.7%), Brennan ($9,400, 39.6%), and the bold third option, defending champion Young ($10,900, 9.6%). Kim and Brennan are the near-identical pair (the anchor-equivalence check) — with two entries, a sharp puts one entry on each side of that coin flip rather than doubling the same side. Doubling Kim means both entries live or die on the exact bet 4 of 10 opponents already made.
2. **The leverage piece — exactly one.** The codified one-dart rule: every winning lineup carries a low-owned piece, and on a birdie-fest the dose is ONE, not three. Your own history enforces this: your last single entry ran five sub-10% golfers at 7.3% average ownership and finished mid-pack, while that contest's winners averaged 20.5% per spot with fewer than two low-owned pieces. The dart candidates: Young (9.6%), Rai (4.8% — sub-5% spike and 2024 champion in one), Kohles (9.0%), Theegala (7.1%), McCarthy (5.3%), or the $6K shelf (Parry 4.2%, Hodges 3.8%, Olesen 3.4%). At least one of the two entries carrying a true sub-5% name (Rai or the $6K shelf) satisfies the birdie-fest spike rule for the whole portfolio.
3. **What a sharp refuses.** Both halves of the top duplicated pairs: Kim + Hossler (~24 of 141 teams), Kim + Smalley (~24), Kim + Brennan (~23), and the Smalley + Noren twins (~20). Flat payouts soften the penalty for sharing, but carrying two of the 40% group means roughly a sixth of the room starts with your backbone — your remaining spots must out-pick all of them. 4. **The salary shape.** The chalk all fits — that's the trap. Kim + Brennan together cost $19,800, leaving $7,550 per spot for four spots, which pushes a build straight into the crowded $7K tier and away from the empty $8,600–$9,200 tier. The field's 10/9/8/7/7/7 shape is a choice, not a law.
5. **Two contests, two different entries.** The contests are identical twins (141 entrants, flat payout), so field size can't differentiate them — the entries themselves must. The sharp's split: one entry is the modal-shape bet — 3 leaderboard golfers, roughly 19–23% average ownership per spot, one dart — that wins if the leaderboard simply holds; the other flips the anchor twin and/or drops to 2 leaderboard golfers with a premium chaser (Hideki/JT/Koivun tier), and wins if Sunday scoring reopens and a chaser posts the day's big number. One question each, answered once.
6. **The pre-lock check.** Ask each built entry: Does it carry at least one sub-10% golfer (and does one of the two entries carry a sub-5%)? Is its average ownership per spot inside roughly 16–23% (the observed shark envelope is 16.3%; your last SE winners ran ~20%)? Does it hold 2 or 3 leaderboard golfers — never 1, never 4? Does it avoid carrying both halves of a top pair (Kim+Hossler, Kim+Smalley, Smalley+Noren)? Does it hold no more than 2 golfers in the 10–20% ownership band (the mid-tier trap)? Can you say in one sentence how it wins?

The one-line target: six golfers averaging roughly 16–23% ownership, one dart, 2–3 leaderboard starters, both entries unique and answering different questions. The coaching note from your own contests: moklovin has beaten you 3 of 7 times running ~22.9% per spot — comfortable chalk discipline, not hero picks. Your recurring gap on both graded Round 4 slates was running LIGHTER than the winning shape, not heavier — the sharp move this week is disciplined chalk plus exactly one bet the room isn't making.
