### The shark gap (the structural you-versus-the-pros read)

This compares the shape of your entry against the tracked pros in the same contest. The axis where the pros separated most: **average ownership per roster spot.** Your SE entry averaged 7.3% ownership per golfer — meaning your typical pick was on fewer than 1 in 13 teams. The pro in that field (moklovin) averaged 22.9% and finished top 1%. That is a gap of 15.6 ownership points per spot. You also had zero exposure to all three chalk anchors (Young, Kirk, Brennan); the pro carried one.

**This is a RECURRING leak — the sharpest kind.** On 6/19 the same axis separated: you took the #3-projected golfer (Fitzpatrick) to zero while the pro carried him at 19%, and your two most contrarian builds finished dead last. The mechanism, stated plainly: **in this chalkier format, I keep building more contrarian than the winners' proven shape, and the pros beat me by simply owning the right amount of the popular golfers.** The twist this slate: your 34-entry set actually FIXED this (14.85% average — near the envelope, best finish top 7.1%), but the single SE bullet regressed to the old habit. That contrast birthed a new lesson (see below).

## What this means for next slate

This section is the takeaway list — most important first.

1. Your single-entry bullet must look like the winning shape — six golfers averaging about 19–23% ownership with one or two low-owned pieces — because with one bullet there is no portfolio to hold the normal outcome for you.
2. An "own less" call (UNDERWEIGHT) in a one-entry contest still means OWN SOME — never let it silently become zero, because that is how you zeroed the slate's top scorer.
3. Keep the leverage screen exactly as it ran this slate — it named both contest-winning low-owned carriers before lock, and covering all 12 candidates is the process that fixed the old 0% capture leak.
4. Popular golfers with a real ceiling and no article coverage are a numbers case to own, not a fade — the article's silence is not evidence.
5. When the course is giving up low scores, cheap golfers high on the leaderboard are real value even when their ownership is money-driven — the "salary-relief trap" story lost again.
