**1. Si Woo Kim** — $10,000, 17.2% owned (DK Round 4 Showdown projection file), proj 37.3

He is the seventh-best projected score on the whole board. Only about 1 in 6 teams will have him. Every golfer projected above him is owned more, except Hideki Matsuyama. The ETR article names him one of "the 4 guys I'll be focused on," and expects his real ownership to land under the projection. That is the rare case where the write-up and the numbers agree. The risk is that he is still a $10,000 golfer, which eats a fifth of your salary cap. — Core

**2. Patrick Cantlay** — $9,800, 21.7% owned, proj 39.0

He has the third-best projected score on the board at $9,800. About 1 in 5 teams is expected to have him. But the article says he "has come down in ownership in almost every R4 he's played this year. I don't see why that wouldn't be the case this time around." That means the golfer you actually get is likely cheaper in crowd-cost than the file says. His ceiling path is simple — he is one of the steadiest scorers in the field on a course giving up 7-under rounds. — Core

**3. Michael Brennan** — $8,600, 37.6% owned, proj 38.0

He starts the day T-2, one spot behind the leader. He is the cheapest golfer in the field with a real chance to win the tournament outright. That matters because the ETR historical chart shows 72.2% of the best Showdown lineups contained the actual tournament winner. The article says ETR's Sky "will tell you that he's a perfect fit for this course," and that his ownership could "come up to the 40s." The catch is the crowd — at 37.6% owned, about 4 of every 10 teams have him, so being right on him pays you the same as everybody else. Our own rules also say taking a top anchor to zero is a voluntary exit from the contest, not leverage. — Core

**4. Hideki Matsuyama** — $9,600, 9.1% owned, proj 36.1

He is the highest-projected golfer on the entire board owned under 10%. Only about 1 in 11 teams will have him. His path to a huge day is straightforward — he is a major champion who can post a very low round when a course is soft. If he does, almost nobody shares him with you. The one warning is a direct disagreement. The article says flatly, "I won't be playing Deki/Koivun... I just don't think 9% ownership on either of them is reasonable." So the numbers call him the best low-owned golfer available and the writer says he should be owned even less. — Core · Leverage

**5. Xander Schauffele** — $10,400, 26.0% owned, proj 39.1

About 1 in 4 teams will have him. The knock is not the golf. It is that Si Woo Kim is nearly the same bet, $400 cheaper, at about 9 fewer points of crowd. — Core

**6. Cameron Young** — $11,000, 32.6% owned, proj 40.7

He is the highest-projected golfer on the board at 40.7 points. He is also the most expensive at $11,000. The article's verdict is lukewarm — "Young is ok but expensive." About 1 in 3 teams will have him. The real cost is downstream. Spending $11,000 leaves $39,000 for five golfers, which is $7,800 each, and that math is exactly what pushes the field into the cheap $6,000 tier. — Core

If you want that price shelf, Henley is the cheaper-crowd half of one bet. — Core
