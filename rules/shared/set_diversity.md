# Set Diversity Rule — build loose, pick strict

**User directive 2026-08-30. Applies cross-sport, any time more than one lineup is entered on a slate.**

## The doctrine

The division of labor is fixed:

- **The opto (Sim tool) BUILDS.** It runs LOOSE on purpose — a big pool with minimal rules, so the pool holds many different answers to the slate. Claude never authors a roster. (This is why the builder's default is "differ by 1 player" and why most build-time rules were deliberately removed.)
- **Claude PICKS.** The stringent rules live at selection time, not build time: Claude selects real rows from the already-built pool that fit this slate's strategy, and matches each pick to the contest it fits best. Picking to fit criteria is not building — the nuance that makes this legal under "Selection is not construction."
- **Strict is still a guide, not a gate.** Rules steer the pick and rank the candidates; they never auto-delete lineups (8/29/26: a hard gate deleted both contest winners over one LEAN FADE call). Break a rule only with a logged override.

## Why it exists

**Lack of diversity is a secret killer of lineup sets.** Every lineup can look great on its own while the set is secretly one bet made several times. That risk is invisible per-lineup — it only shows up when you measure the SET. Over-concentration hides even in micro sets: two 3-max entries sharing 5 of 6 players is ~one entry paying double fees. And duplication is a measured tax: a lineup the field also built splits the prize before the slate even starts (large-field analysis shows dupe avoidance is very valuable; winning lineups run 85–91% unique).

## How to apply — the set check (before any multi-lineup entry is finalized)

Diversity is measured **multiple layers down**, not just "no exact copies":

1. **Player layer.** Count shared players between every pair of chosen lineups. In small-field sets (SE/3-max/5-max), flag any pair sharing more than 4 of 6. Existing sport rules are instances of this one rule: MMA two-SE must differ on a high-conviction play, RD4 SD 2-2 rule-set split, NASCAR "each lineup has unique identity," 3-max 2/3 and 5-max 3/5 exposure caps.
2. **Combo layer.** Look one layer deeper than players: flag any 2- or 3-player combo appearing in more than half the set. Two lineups that differ by one name but share the same core pair are the same bet wearing different hats.
3. **Thesis layer.** Every lineup keeps its one-sentence "how it wins" thesis, and no two lineups in the set answer the same question (existing rules — restated here because thesis distinctness is the top diversity layer).
4. **Dupe layer.** Expected duplicates in the field ≈ 0 for every entry: ownership product × field size (MMA runs 1.7–6.6× superlinear on that estimate; leaving $500–1,000 of salary unspent collapses dupe risk). One sub-5% piece usually does it.
5. **Contest fit.** Say which lineup goes in which contest and why — one lineup, one contest; the biggest-money contest gets first claim on the best-fitting lineup.

## Scope notes

- Do not swing to over-diversification: with a clear slate read, fewer distinct cores rostered deeply beats max breadth (RD4 SD codified warning). Diversity means distinct ANSWERS, not forced randomness.
- 150-max/MME sets legitimately share 5/6 players between neighbors — there the layers that bind are combo concentration, exposure caps, and dupes, not pairwise overlap.
