# NFL Classic Philosophy — Ryan's GPP Approach

**Seeded 2026-09-12 from docs/nfl_game_theory.md Part 1 + docs/nfl_support_scope.md contest profile. Updated 2026-09-12 with the ETR Classic research digest. User-approved 2026-09-12.**

## Core identity

GPP only, playing to WIN, never to cash. In Classic that means: the game stack IS the lineup — which game, which quarterback, how many of his pass-catchers, and whether a player from the other side rides along. Everything else (the running backs, the defense, the tight end) fills in around that bet. The user's Classic profile is **the smallest fields the bankroll allows** (single-entry / 3-Max / 5-Max, fields of a few hundred to a few thousand): the same SE/3-Max home game as golf, MMA and NASCAR, judged on ceiling only.

## The six beliefs this format runs on

1. **Points come in bundles, so buy the bundle.** A touchdown pass scores twice on your roster when the thrower and the catcher are both there. Stacking costs no salary and no median projection; it fattens the right tail, and GPPs are won in the tail. The measured edge is the DOUBLE stack (QB + 2 of his own pass-catchers: 41% of winners vs 29% of the field) with a bring-back (one pass-catcher from the other team). A quarterback with no teammates is a losing shape (6% of winners vs 17% of the field).
2. **Pick the game before the players.** Correlation runs through games, so the slate read starts with the Vegas total and spread, not with a player list. The richest leverage in football is a low-owned stack, not a low-owned player: the field floods the highest total, so the second or third highest total with the same shape at half the ownership is where the edge lives.
3. **Chalk has two kinds, and the position tells you which.** Sturdy chalk is built on volume (a bell-cow running back with 20 touches) and running back is the one position where winners were CHALKIER than the field — eat it. Fragile chalk is built on last week's highlight, a touchdown-dependent role, or a popular defense; winners diverge from the field hardest at QB, DST and TE. A chalk defense is the most reliably overpriced thing on any slate.
4. **Spend the cap; differentiate with structure.** 84% of winners spent $49,800 or more, and leaving salary has no measured edge in Classic (the opposite of Showdown). Uniqueness comes from player choices, the double stack, and the tight-end bring-back — structure is the cheapest uniqueness there is.
5. **Small fields want the same lineup, not a safer one.** The user's own 28 small-field contests show the same winner as a Milly Maker: ~94% total ownership, about four players under 10% owned, two under 5%, one ~30% anchor, and a unique roster. What changes with field size is the field (chalkier, softer), not the shape that wins. Rates describe winners after the fact — no rate is ever a per-lineup quota.
6. **The sim is a threshold, and the pick is made inside it.** ETR's own calibration on a ~275-entry single-entry field (35 contests): positive-sim lineups win 1.65x as often as chance, negative-sim 0.57x, and the three best sim buckets post the same real ROI. So: build to the threshold, then choose by ownership robustness (the three checks) and the one-sentence thesis — never by the exact ROI order. "AVG" and "CEILING" points do not matter; beating the field does, and some weeks that is a low-scoring slate.

## What never changes (house rules, all sports)

- The Analyzer synthesizes and names individual plays in tiers; it NEVER builds lineups.
- A trap is a price, not a player. Every fade cites THIS slate's numbers.
- Only a call this slate's strategy makes can cost a grade; every statistic is information.
- Best-percentile trend + process metrics are the scoreboard; ROI lives in the third-party app.
