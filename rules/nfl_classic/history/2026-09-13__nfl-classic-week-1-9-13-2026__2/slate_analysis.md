# NFL Classic — Week 1 slate strategy (Sunday, September 13, 2026)

_DraftKings main slate, 12 games. Sources: ten Establish The Run (ETR) pieces dated Sep 9–12 (Silva's Matchups, the Rundown, DFS Top Plays, GPP Leverage, Macro vs. Micro, Cheap WR Volume, GPP Game Scores, Snaps and Pace, Thorn's OL vs. DL Mismatches, the Establish The Million show notes) plus the ETR DraftKings projection sheet (340 players). All prices, projections, and ownership numbers below are DraftKings numbers from that sheet unless an article is named._

## The short version

This section is the whole slate in plain words, for someone who has never played DFS.

1. This is a big 12-game Sunday where one game (Saints at Lions) is the crowd's favorite and one player (Jahmyr Gibbs) will be on about 6 of every 10 teams.
2. Points in football come in bundles: when a quarterback throws a touchdown, the catcher scores too, so the winning team is built around one game and its quarterback, not around single players.
3. The crowd is stacking the Saints–Lions game and filling the cheap tight end spot with Michael Mayer, who costs almost nothing because the Raiders' star tight end got hurt midweek.
4. The one decision that separates entries this week is the quarterback: five quarterbacks project within about one point of each other, and the crowd picked the most obvious one, Jared Goff, at four times the ownership of Jalen Hurts.
5. A sharp keeps Gibbs (his workload is real), then spends the ownership savings on a quarterback-and-receivers group from a game the crowd is skipping, such as Bengals–Buccaneers, Bears–Panthers, Commanders–Eagles, or Cardinals–Chargers.
6. The biggest trap is paying for a defense because it is popular: the Jets defense is the second-most-picked defense on the slate but only the eighth-best by projection.
7. Every lineup should spend the full $50,000, because leftover money buys nothing here and every winning lineup is already different from every other one.
8. In the 3-entry contest, the three lineups should tell three different game stories, never three versions of the same build.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that actually decide the slate, with the number that reveals each one.

1. **The crowd's game is not the highest-total game.** Saints–Lions is the top ETR game score (3.0) and the highest-owned game, yet Bucs–Bengals carries the higher total (50.5 vs 49.5) with Joe Burrow at 11.3% owned against Jared Goff's 19.3%.
2. **Five quarterbacks project the same; the crowd picked one.** Burrow 21.8, Hurts 20.9, Herbert 20.9, Goff 20.5, Lamar Jackson 20.3 — owned 11.3%, 4.6%, 10.0%, 19.3%, 5.6%. That is the Anchor-Equivalence observation at the quarterback level: Goff has no projection edge over the four cheaper-owned twins.
3. **The second-running-back tier is one bet in four jerseys.** De'Von Achane (20.0 proj, 16.2%), Omarion Hampton (18.5, 17.5%), Saquon Barkley (18.2, 16.1%), and Jonathan Taylor (19.8, 14.6%) sit within $500 and 3 ownership points; Bijan Robinson projects higher than all four (20.5) at 10.2% owned.
4. **The forced-value play is the field's whole salary plan.** Michael Mayer at $2,900 is 47.8% owned because Brock Bowers was ruled out midweek; ETR's Top Plays says he had a 25% target share in four healthy games without Bowers, so the role is locked and the field will eat him.
5. **The dupe magnet has a name.** The field pairs Gibbs + Mayer in about 28% of lineups, roughly 696 of 2,450 teams in the single-entry, and Gibbs + Ja'Marr Chase in about 19%, roughly 461 teams (co-occurrence estimated from ownership).
6. **The best low-owned stack is in Charlotte, not Detroit.** Caleb Williams is 5.5% owned with a 19.1 projection in an up-in-pace game where Thorn ranks the Bears' line 7th against the Panthers' 26th-ranked pass rush (the week's second-biggest offensive-line edge); Silva writes "both Loveland and Burden can smash here."
7. **Popular defenses are owned far ahead of their projection.** The Jets defense ($2,500) is 18.4% owned with a 7.3 projection, 126 ownership ranks ahead of where it projects; the Steelers ($3,300, 4.5%) project higher (7.8) with Thorn's third-biggest pass-rush mismatch and a backup quarterback across from them.
8. **Vendor versus article on Derrick Henry.** The ETR sheet projects Henry 18.2 at 13.4% owned; Silva calls the Ravens' interior line "a glaring personnel disadvantage" against the Colts' tackles, while Leone flag-plants Henry on the Million show. That gap is unresolved and it is the week's clearest article-versus-article split.
9. **Week 1 charges a price for leverage.** Brott's GPP Leverage study found the higher-owned of two matched twins outscored the lower-owned one about 60% of the time in Week 1 (Milly Maker), so the field's preference carries information this week more than it will in November.
10. **Three cheap chalk pieces are owned ahead of their projection.** Devaughn Vele ($3,500, 14.3%, proj 9.3), Juwan Johnson ($3,700, 16.8%, proj 11.1), and Tre Tucker ($4,000, 18.1%, proj 11.6) are the trap-shaped prices this week; each is a fine piece only inside the stack he belongs to.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section shows the two pictures side by side: what the crowd's typical team looks like and what a sharp's team looks like.

**How the FIELD will play it.** The typical entry carries Jahmyr Gibbs (59.5%) and Michael Mayer (47.8%), then spends the savings on Ja'Marr Chase (31.6%) or Chris Olave (28.3%) and Amon-Ra St. Brown (27.2%), with Jared Goff (19.3%) as the quarterback and a popular defense (Jets 18.4%, Jaguars 16.6%). Dinkmeyer said it on the Million show: Saints–Lions "will be the highest-owned game without a doubt," and the second running-back slot rotates among Hampton, Achane, Barkley, and Taylor at 14–18% each.

**How a SHARP will play it.** The observed sharp envelope for your NFL contests is about 13.7% average ownership per roster slot, a sub-5%-owned piece in about 88% of their lineups (a rate across many entries, never a per-lineup rule), and 98.6% of lineups unique. Applied here: a sharp keeps Gibbs, because running-back ownership is the sturdy kind (ETR's winner data says winners are chalkier than the field at running back), and creates separation at quarterback and in the stack, taking Burrow, Hurts, Herbert, or Caleb Williams over Goff and pairing that quarterback with two of his own pass-catchers. The sub-5% piece comes from the quarterback tier (Hurts 4.6%, Josh Allen 0.7%) or a bring-back nobody carries (Trey McBride 1.3%, Emeka Egbuka 4.9%, Dallas Goedert 4.6%).

**The gap.** The field pays 19.3% ownership for Goff's 20.5 projection and stacks him with the two most obvious Lions; the sharp gets the same projection at 4.6% (Hurts) or 11.3% (Burrow) and stacks it with a double stack the crowd is not building. Both sides own Gibbs; the argument is settled downstream of him.

- **Jared Goff ($6,000, 19.3%) — UNDERWEIGHT** — his 20.5 projection ranks fourth among quarterbacks while his ownership ranks first; Hurts (20.9, 4.6%) and Herbert (20.9, 10.0%) project higher for less crowd, and Leone said he "will look at getting away from Goff in small field because of the steam."

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes on this slate, in the order they make them. It names candidates for each decision; the user picks.

1. **The game-and-quarterback decision.** A sharp picks the game first, and the quarterback is the anchor of that pick: Burrow ($6,900, 11.3%) in the 50.5 game with Chase and Tee Higgins ($6,300, 13.1%); Hurts ($6,600, 4.6%) with DeVonta Smith and Goedert ($4,100, 4.6%); Herbert ($6,100, 10.0%) with Ladd McConkey ($5,900, 11.9%) and Hampton; or Caleb Williams ($6,300, 5.5%) with Loveland ($5,300, 13.6%) and Burden ($5,500, 11.5%). Hurts and Lamar are twins at 4.6% and 5.6%; Herbert and Burrow are twins at 10.0% and 11.3%; Goff is the popular twin of all four at 19.3%.
2. **Double stack, then the bring-back.** The measured edge is quarterback plus two of his own catchers (41% of winners vs 29% of the field); a sharp adds one player from the other side when the game is a shootout: Egbuka ($6,200, 4.9%) or Chris Godwin Jr. ($5,200, 8.4%) for a Burrow stack, McMillan ($6,100, 9.4%) or Coker ($4,600, 5.5%) for a Williams stack, McBride ($6,800, 1.3%) for a Herbert stack, Chig Okonkwo ($4,000, 0.2%) for a Hurts stack. Where the line says blowout (Chargers −9.5, Eagles −6), Leone's read applies: the bring-back is optional.
3. **What the field duplicates.** Gibbs + Mayer sits in about 696 of the 2,450 single-entry teams and about 557 of the 1,960 three-max teams; Gibbs + Chase in about 461 and 369. That count is information, not a rule: the two are in different games and both can pay on one Sunday, so a sharp does not refuse the pair. What a sharp does refuse is the third piece of the copy: Gibbs + Mayer + Goff-to-St. Brown + the Jets or Jaguars defense at exactly $50,000 is the one lineup hundreds of opponents already built.
4. **The running-back decision.** Gibbs is the anchor at every ownership, because running-back chalk built on volume is the kind winners carry (Leone has him in 3 of 4 high-stakes lineups). The second back is where a sharp separates: Bijan ($7,700, 10.2%) projects above the four-back twin tier; Cook ($7,200, 4.6%) and Chase Brown ($7,100, 8.3%) are the cheaper-owned versions of the same projection band. A running back in the flex is the winning shape (58% of winners), and Leone notes "top lineups are using more 3 RB than the field" this week.
5. **The salary shape.** Gibbs ($8,000) plus Mayer ($2,900) is $10,900 for two spots, leaving $39,100 for seven spots, about $5,586 each; adding Chase ($7,800) leaves $31,300 for six, about $5,217 each. That is the whole field's math, and it is why every lineup lands on the same $5,000–$6,000 receivers. A sharp who drops Mayer for McBride ($6,800) or Loveland ($5,300) has to give back a stud elsewhere, and the stud to give back is the one the field has at 27–32% (St. Brown or Chase), never Gibbs. Spend to $49,800 or more; 84% of winners did.
6. **The defense.** A sharp grades a defense by pressure, never by points allowed (Thorn: only 2.5% of games are shutouts). The pressure board this week: Eagles ($3,200, 4.8%) hold Thorn's No. 1 mismatch against a Commanders line missing Laremy Tunsil; Steelers ($3,300, 4.5%) hold No. 3 against Cooper Rush; Jaguars ($3,400, 16.6%) hold No. 2 and are the top-projected defense (9.8); Jets hold No. 6 at 18.4%. A defense never faces the lineup's own stack, and a favorite's defense next to its running back (Eagles with Barkley, Steelers with Warren) is a mild positive pair.
7. **Four entries, four different stories.** The single-entry (2,450 teams) wants the highest-ceiling correlated story the field is not building: a Burrow double stack or a Williams double stack with Gibbs. The 3-max (1,960 teams) wants three different game stories, never three shades of one: one Lions build through the less obvious pieces (Goff with Jameson Williams $6,000, 13.5% and Juwan Johnson as the Saints bring-back), one Bengals–Bucs build, and one from the Hurts / Herbert / Williams / Kyler-Jefferson group. At least one of the four entries runs a quarterback other than Goff; that is the Anchor-Equivalence rule at the game level.
8. **The pre-lock check.** Does the lineup name its stack shape (quarterback + how many teammates, bring-back or not) and the game story behind it? Is the defense facing the lineup's own quarterback? Is it the exact copy from step 3 (Gibbs + Mayer + Goff-to-St. Brown + Jets or Jaguars defense at $50,000)? Does it spend $49,800 or more? In the 3-max, do the three entries tell three different game stories?

The sharp-envelope target in one line: about 13.7% average ownership per slot, a sub-5% piece somewhere across the set (not in every lineup), every lineup unique — youdacao, the strongest converter among the tracked NFL pros (top-1% finish 3.4% of the time), runs 14.7% per slot with a leverage piece in 82% of entries, so a Gibbs-plus-Mayer lineup with a low-owned quarterback stack sits squarely inside that envelope.
