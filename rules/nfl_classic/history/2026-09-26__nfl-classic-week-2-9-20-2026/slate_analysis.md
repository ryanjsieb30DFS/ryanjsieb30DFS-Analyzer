# NFL Classic — Week 2 slate strategy (Sunday, September 20, 2026)

_DraftKings only. Every FanDuel number in the source articles was ignored._

## The short version

1. This is a 13-game Sunday football slate where you pick nine players under one salary cap.
2. Points come in bundles: when a quarterback throws a touchdown, the receiver who catches it scores too, so a quarterback plus two of his own pass-catchers (a double stack) is how winners get built.
3. Washington at Dallas is the highest-scoring game on paper, and it is where most of the crowd will put its quarterback and receivers.
4. The crowd also piles onto one running back, Bijan Robinson, in almost four of every ten lineups, and onto a cheap tight end, Dalton Schultz, in three of ten.
5. The one decision that separates entries is which game you stack, because two or three other games offer the same shootout shape at half the crowd's interest.
6. A sharp keeps the popular running back, moves the quarterback to a less-crowded game like Jayden Daniels, Brock Purdy or Lamar Jackson, and takes a tight end and a defense almost nobody else has.
7. The biggest trap is paying for popularity at tight end and defense, where the crowd's picks project no better than the ignored ones.
8. With three entries, each one should tell a different game story, never three versions of one build.

## Edges & tensions

This section lists the things that matter most this week, with the number that shows each one.

1. **The stack game is the decision.** WAS @ DAL is the top total (51.5), the fastest game, and ETR's top game score (2.6); MIA @ SF (2.2), MIN @ CHI (1.1) and NO @ BAL (1.0) are the pivots.

## Field vs Sharp — how this slate gets played

This section describes what the typical entry looks like and what a sharp entry looks like, so you know what you are building against.

**How the field plays it.** The typical lineup starts with Bijan Robinson (37.5%), adds one of Henry, Jones or Javonte (28 to 30% each), fills tight end with Schultz (31.7%) or Andrews (20.0%), and stacks Dak with Lamb (28.6%) and Pickens (25.9%), which The Show calls "the most popular stack in tournaments." Its defense is the Buccaneers (12.7%) or Ravens (10.9%), and Golden (17.3%) or Garrett Wilson (17.4%) fills the last receiver spot.

**How a sharp plays it.** Your tracked pros in these fields average about 14% ownership per slot, carry a player under 5% owned in most lineups, and are unique 99% of the time; youdacao (17.6% per slot, a sub-5% piece in most lineups) is the closest model. Applied here, a sharp keeps Bijan or CMC (19.7%) as the running-back anchor, moves the quarterback to Daniels, Purdy, Lamar or Nix at 3 to 8%, takes a tight end from the 1 to 7% band (Goedert, Juwan Johnson, Freiermuth, Kittle), and picks a defense backed by a pass-rush mismatch (49ers 4.4%, Eagles 4.8%, Seahawks 4.2%).

**The gap.** The field pays 31.7% ownership for Schultz's 11.6 projection and 12.7% for the Buccaneers' 9.3; the sharp gets 16.7 from McBride at 7.3% and 9.1 from the 49ers at 4.4%. At quarterback the field sits on Dak (15.9%) and Wentz (11.1%); the sharp gets a 19-to-21-point projection from Daniels, Purdy or Lamar at a third of that ownership.

## Build it like a sharp

This section walks through the decisions a sharp makes, in order, for a single-entry player who wants to copy the pros. Candidates are named; you pick.

1. **The game-stack decision.** A sharp picks the game first: Dak (15.9%) with Lamb + Pickens and McLaurin (14.2%) or Diggs (7.0%) as the bring-back is the modal build; Daniels (7.8%) with McLaurin + Diggs and one Dallas receiver back is the same shootout at half the quarterback ownership; Purdy (7.0%) with Evans (6.1%) or Kittle (6.3%) needs no bring-back as a 10.5-point favorite; Lamar (3.2%) with Bateman (0.6%) or Andrews and Olave (5.8%) or Juwan Johnson (7.1%) back is the up-in-pace pivot. At running back the anchor is Bijan (37.5%) or CMC (19.7%), never the defense facing the lineup's own quarterback.
2. **The leverage piece.** A sharp's sub-5% player comes from Hurts (1.0%), Caleb (2.0%), Goedert (1.5%), Bateman (0.6%), Egbuka (2.6%) or Saquon (8.7%); youdacao carries one in most lineups, not all.
3. **What the field duplicates.** Bijan + Schultz sits in about 187 lineups and Bijan + Henry in about 179; carrying either pair means sharing the entry with that many opponents before kickoff. Lamb + Pickens is fine inside a Dak stack because Silva's read says both pay in the same script; Henry + Jones + Javonte are one bet, so a sharp holds one of the three.
4. **The salary shape.** Bijan $8,200 + Dak $6,400 + Lamb $7,300 + Pickens $6,300 = $28,200, leaving $21,800 for five spots, about $4,360 each, which is exactly what forces Schultz and Jones into the lineup. Daniels $6,300 + McLaurin $5,200 + Diggs $5,300 + Bijan $8,200 = $25,000, leaving $5,000 a spot, which fits Henry or Saquon as the second back. The real decision is Javonte OR the second Dallas receiver; the full cap ($49,800 or more) gets spent either way.
5. **Three entries, three stories.** One contest is declared, so the split is by game story: one WAS @ DAL stack, one MIA @ SF or NO @ BAL stack, and one cheap-quarterback stack (Nix, Lawrence, Love or Baker); no two entries share two or more pieces from one game, and no quarterback appears twice.
6. **Big-field attack.** Not written; no 20-Max or 150-Max is declared.
7. **The pre-lock check.** Does each quarterback have two of his own pass-catchers? Is the defense not facing the lineup's own quarterback? Is the lineup under six players from WAS @ DAL? If it carries Bijan + Schultz, is there a reason beyond salary? Across the three entries, do any two share two pieces from one game?

Target envelope: about 14% ownership per slot, a sub-5% piece in most but not every lineup, unique; coaching from youdacao (17.6% per slot, beat you in 2 of 6 slates).
