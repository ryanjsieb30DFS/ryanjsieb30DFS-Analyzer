## Physical profile

- **Length:** 2.5 miles, rectangular, long straightaways with heavy braking into flat 90-degree corners
- **Banking:** 6–14 degrees (flat)
- **Race length:** ~160 laps — short lap count relative to a 300-lap intermediate, so fewer total dominator points exist
- **Cautions:** last 4 Indy Cup races averaged **8.5 cautions for 38.25 caution laps** (DDD). 2024 "wrecked half the field"; 2025 saw Katherine Legge finish 17th purely on variance

## Comparables

Per DDD there is **no true comparable track**. **Pocono is the closest** (long frontstretch, flat banking, tunnel turn designed with Indy in mind). Michigan / Kansas / Fontana are "a bit of a stretch" on speed and momentum alone. Flat-track performance is only partly transferable — speeds here are far higher than a short flat like North Wilkesboro.

## Strategic notes (from the 7.26.26 articles)

- **Track position over everything.** DDD's stated disclaimer for the slate: nobody has passed the leader under green here, and you do not pass multiple cars on a long run unless you are exceptionally fast. Two-tire stops, restart execution, and pit strategy create the finishing order more than raw speed does.
- **Optimal-lineup start distribution, last 4 Indy Cup races (image-35):** Top-5 start = 12.5% of optimal slots · Top-10 = 12.5% · **11th–20th = 20.8%** · **21st–30th = 45.8% (the meat)** · worse-than-30th = 20.8%. Top-10 DK scores: Top-5 15.0% / Top-10 25.0% / 11–20 22.5% / **21–30 40.0%** / worse-than-30th 12.5%.
- **Indy + Pocono combined, L4 each (image-36):** 21–30 = 32.5% of top-10 DK scores and 37.5% of optimal slots; 11–20 = 23.8% / 25.0%; worse-than-30th = 11.3% / 16.7%. The 11–30 band is ~62% of optimal across both venues.
- **Deep-back is live here, unlike Pocono.** Worse-than-30th is 20.8% of Indy optimal slots (vs only 9.5% at Pocono) — Cindric went P38→7th for 68.0 points as the top score in the 2024 optimal, and Cody Ware finished 18th from the back in 2024. Do not import Pocono's thin-deep-back read to Indy.
- **Dominator points are thin and concentrated at the pole.** Laps led by top-10 starters (image-40): P1 led 34 (2025), 40 (2024), 14 (2020), 118 (2019); P2 led 30 / 21 / 0 / 1. Everyone else in the top 10 is sporadic. DDD: 30 laps led is only 7.5 extra points — another driver clears it by gaining 4 positions.
- **Place differential is the scoring engine.** In the 2025 optimal, Hamlin led **1 lap** and scored 81.3 on 36 spots of PD from P39; JHN scored 55.7 on 24 PD. In 2024, Cindric scored 68.0 with **zero** laps led. DDD: "place differential is the safer route, especially in a race with just 160 laps." One to two dominators, not three.
- **Chalk pivots have paid.** 2025: Hamlin and JHN were the two highest-owned drivers and both were optimal (justified chalk), but Chase Elliott and Ross Chastain were both 30%+ owned from P30/P33 and neither was optimal (Elliott 13th, Chastain crashed lap 17). 2024: Reddick was 49% owned from the pole, led 40 laps, finished 2nd — and still was not optimal at $8,800, while Suarez (5.54%), Gilliland (7.67%) and Wallace delivered from similar starts.
- **Perceived equipment advantage is weaker here than elsewhere.** DDD: "At Indy where track position is so vital, a perceived difference in equipment isn't always the advantage we think it is."
