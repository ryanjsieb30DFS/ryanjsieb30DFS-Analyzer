# Richmond Raceway

**UNVERIFIED — built from this slate's articles only (2026-08-15). No autopsy observations yet.**

## Profile

- 0.75-mile flat tri-oval, low banking, **high tire wear**. Comparable tracks: Phoenix, New Hampshire, Gateway, and to a lesser extent Martinsville (Dustin's Deep Dive, 8/15/26).
- Richmond and Phoenix are described as reverse versions of each other — speed is carried by momentum through the corners onto the straights. New Hampshire/Martinsville are braking-rhythm tracks; Richmond is not.
- **400 laps scheduled, ~90% run green → ≈262.5 dominator points available** (DDD). Picking the right dominators is the slate.

## Dominator structure

- DDD targets a **minimum of two dominators**; one driver rarely leads the whole race.
- **11 of the past 12 Richmond optimal lineups carried at least 2 drivers who each led 50+ laps** (DDD).
- NextGen trend: fast laps spread wider through the field — **5 of the past 6 optimal lineups had 4+ drivers with double-digit fast laps** (DDD).
- Tire degradation means fast laps churn as cars cycle through pit stops — real fast-lap variance for off-sequence cars.

## Start-position bands (DFR chart, past 12 races)

| Start band | Share of top-10 DK scores | Share of optimal-lineup spots |
|---|---|---|
| Top 5 | 21.7% | 19.4% |
| Top 10 | 40.8% | 31.9% |
| 11th–20th | 27.5% | 19.4% |
| **21st–30th** | 25.8% | **36.1% (largest bucket)** |
| Worse than 30th | 5.8% | 12.5% |

Read: raw scoring concentrates up front (top-10 starters = 41% of top-10 DK scores, DDD), but **salary-adjusted optimal slots come mostly from the 21st–30th band** — consistent with the codified mid-pack-PD-is-the-meat lesson.

- Only 7 times in 12 races has a worse-than-30th starter made a top-10 DK score, and every one came from above-average-to-elite equipment (DDD).
- True punts made the optimal lineup only twice in 12 races, both times to fit two $11,000+ dominators (DDD).
- Passing is hard; back-of-field cars are often lapped before the end of stage 1, which caps deep place-differential gains (DDD).

## Variance

Less wreck-driven variance than other short tracks — good for DFS predictability. The variance that exists comes from tire wear and off-sequence fast laps (DDD).
