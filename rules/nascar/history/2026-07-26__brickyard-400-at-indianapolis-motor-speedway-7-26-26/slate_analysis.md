# NASCAR — Brickyard 400, Indianapolis Motor Speedway (7/26/26)

## The short version

1. This is a track-position race, which means where a driver starts matters more than how fast his car is.
2. Nobody has ever passed the leader under green here, so drivers gain spots on pit road and on restarts instead.
3. Almost all the points come from place differential — the points a driver earns for finishing ahead of where he started.
4. Leading laps pays very little this week because the race is short and the leader almost never changes on track.
5. History here says the best scores come from drivers who started deep in the pack, not up front.
6. The crowd has piled onto the two cheapest drivers starting near the very back of the field.
7. The six most popular drivers cannot all fit under the salary cap, so every entry is forced to drop one of them.
8. Which one you drop is the decision that separates your entry from everyone else's this week.
9. The biggest trap is a popular driver whose own preview writer says his car was slow in practice.

## Edges & tensions

**The scoring engine is place differential, not leading laps.** Place differential is the points a driver earns for finishing ahead of his starting spot. Dustin's Deep Dive puts it in plain arithmetic: 30 laps led is only 7.5 extra points. Another driver beats that by gaining four positions.

**Dominator points are real but they live almost entirely in one seat — the pole.** A dominator is a driver who leads laps and runs the fastest laps. The laps-led-by-top-10-starters table shows the pole sitter led 34, 40, 14 and 118 laps in the last four runnings here. Every other top-10 starter was sporadic or led nothing at all.

**The richest starting band is 21st through 30th, and the field is only half aware of it.** The Indy last-four-races chart shows that band supplied 45.8% of all ideal lineup slots. Ten drivers start in that band this week. They range from Christopher Bell at 38% ownership down to Ricky Stenhouse Jr and Josh Berry at 4%.

**Anchor-Equivalence at the top: three near-identical drivers sit within three points of ownership.** Anchor-equivalence means the field treats two similar plays as interchangeable, so the less-owned one carries the same upside with less crowding. Bell ($10,000, 38%), Chase Elliott ($9,500, 36%) and William Byron ($9,700, 35%) all start between 21st and 27th. Dustin ranks Bell 2nd and Byron 6th in five-lap practice average, and calls Elliott mediocre in practice. Same price tier, same starting band, same bet — but the field spreads across all three anyway.

In 2025 John Hunter Nemechek made it from 36th at 53.9% ownership, and Denny Hamlin made it from 39th at 63.4% ownership.
