In today’s article, I’m using the post-lock sims to evaluate one speciﬁc
element of game stacking: whether to include the obvious opposing bring-
back. Several slates last year made me wonder if chalk bring-backs were
overplayed by the ﬁeld . All else equal, more positive correlation improves
a DFS lineup. But all else is not equal. Even good strategies in a vacuum
can turn into -EV strategies if too many of our opponents implement
them. It’s the same reason why the best-projected RB value on a slate could
be a bad tournament play.

When the Chalk Bring-Back Is -EV
With the Chase-Puka dilemma from Week 9 fresh on my mind and
another popular bring-back decision looming in Week 10, I decided to
experiment with playing a more contrarian angle. In the 100-person
Luxury Box, I played a Mac Jones  triple stack, but I opted against the
obvious bring-back ( Puka Nacua ).
In this case, the sims agreed with my decision. You can see below that 10
lineups (10%) in the ﬁeld played Mac Jones . My lineup was the only one
without Nacua. Of the nine Jones stacks with a Nacua bring-back, only
three of them had a post-lock sim ROI that was positive, and those were all
relatively modest (all outside the top 20 ROI teams in a 100-person ﬁeld).
In total, both the mean and median ROIs for the Mac stacks with the chalk
Puka bring-back were negative.

Conclusion
This is a limited study of two small-ﬁeld tournaments from two different
weeks. Still, examining why the sims favored certain lineups produced
several takeaways for the upcoming season:
When Is a Chalk Bring-Back Worth It? | Establish The Run https://establishtherun.com/when-is-a-chalk-bring-back-worth-it/
14 of 16 9/12/26, 8:01 PM
• It’s not always worth forcing the contrarian angle (fading an obvious
bring-back), but it is a tool in the toolbox.
• Adding a chalk bring-back to an existing team stack can be worthwhile,
even at inﬂated ownership, when the player is a strong value and ﬁts
naturally within the roster construction, as Chase did in Week 9.
• Fading a chalk bring-back can create a higher-variance lineup without
sacriﬁcing overall EV, as the Puka-over-Chase pivot did in Week 9.
• Smaller-ﬁeld tournaments may offer clearer opportunities to omit the
chalk bring-back. More opponents hand-build or follow traditional
correlation rules in those contests, and the smaller ﬁeld allows one
such decision to separate you from a meaningful portion of the lineups
you need to beat.
• The strongest bring-back fades are those that unlock a meaningful
roster-construction advantage, as fading Puka did by enabling three
Tier 1 RB values in Week 10.
