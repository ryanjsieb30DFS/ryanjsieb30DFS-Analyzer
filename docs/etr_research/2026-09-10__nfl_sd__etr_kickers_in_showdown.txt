Nothing says NFL showdown quite like Chris Boswell scoring 26 DraftKings
points in an 18-10 win over the Falcons, where the lone offensive highlight was
a 41-yard pass, setting up Boswell’s third field goal of the first half.
You needed a shower at 11:00 p.m. CT to rinse off the stench of that game, but
if you rostered Boswell, you were doing so with a few more dollars in your
pocket. That’s the question this article will answer: Should we be rostering
kickers more in NFL showdown?
Historical ownership pushes back on that assumption: 64.1% of all primetime
NFL showdown lineups in the DraftKings flagship contest roster no kickers. The
position, even in our analysis of them, often gets dismissed as a shrug or the
last piece you click in when you’ve already built a lineup you like. That
dismissal may have worked in the past, but it’s becoming harder to defend now.
The NFL’s kickoff rule was overhauled in 2024 and changed again in 2025,
along with a revamp of the K-Ball preparation rules that have resulted in a
change to how kickers are producing fantasy points, resulting in more field goal
attempts, improved accuracy, expanded range, and better DraftKings scoring
on a per-game basis. Have our opponents priced those things in?
We’ll use our database of 22.4 million weighted contest entries across nearly
200 primetime NFL showdown slates from 2020-2025 to give us an answer for
how the DFS field is handling it, and a play-by-play dataset of the kick detail
covering 5,594 field goal attempts and 6,438 extra point attempts from all NFL
games since 2021 to answer the real-world impact.

What the Data Tells Us
Using the data from all three sources — contest entries, ETR projections and
ownership, and real-game kick detail — we get a nuanced answer to the
question “should we be rostering kickers more?”

Before 2025, the answer was a resounding yes. Lineups that featured a kicker in
FLEX posted a 1.15x win index across more than 15 million contest entries. Our
opponents simply weren’t playing kickers as often as they should.
In 2025, that equation shifted because both the field and DraftKings’ pricing
algorithm caught up. As kicker in FLEX became more popular (37.2% of all
lineups), the median salary increased from $4,000 to $5,000, compressing the
win index to virtually breakeven, despite kicker performance improving.
Playing kickers indiscriminately no longer offers the blanket advantage it once
did as salary and ownership discounts have evaporated.
The real-game data still paints a positive forward-looking picture for the
position, further complicating things. DraftKings scoring per kicker game is the
highest it’s ever been, ceiling game frequency has increased since 2021, and the
K-ball rules have had a meaningful impact on both long-range FG frequency
and success, which means the position is simply better than it was when the
showdown format was created.
What I’ll be watching for in 2026:
Utilization of favored kicker in 5-1 builds: In 2025, 41.6% of all 5-1 lineups
included the favored kicker in FLEX, up from 37.9% prior. The advantage for
playing a kicker in 5-1 builds vs. no kicker was virtually indistinguishable in
2025 after being a common fixture in top-1% finishing lineups in previous
seasons. If the field’s utilization of favored kickers in onslaught builds remains
high, it makes sense to look elsewhere this season.
Dome games: Average kicker DraftKings scoring peaked at 9.2 points in dome
games last season, compared to 7.9 for outdoor games. Dome kickers score 15+
DraftKings points 12.0% of the time compared to 6.7% for outdoor kickers. This
is likely partially priced in by the DFS market, but if kicker ownership is
depressed in these environments, I’ll be more willing to target them
aggressively.
