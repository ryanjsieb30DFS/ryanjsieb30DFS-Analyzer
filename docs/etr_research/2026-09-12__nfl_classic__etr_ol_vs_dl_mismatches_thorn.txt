                    The goal of a real-life NFL defense is to prevent the other team from
                    scoring points. When we select a defense to roster, we’re trying to score the
                    most fantasy points. It’s a subtle shift in thinking, but an important one.

                    The optimal way to select a defense in DFS is to think in terms of big
                    events, not points allowed. Just 2.5% of games last season ended in a
                    shutout, and only 9.7% ended with one team being held to six points or
                    fewer. In other words, targeting the “points allowed” category is a fool’s
                    errand.

                    The real fantasy points come from sacks, forced fumbles, interceptions,
                    and defensive touchdowns. These events are by far most likely to happen
                    when a quarterback is under pressure. The best possible outcome on a
                    single play for our defense is a strip sack that results in a defensive
                    touchdown, as we get one DraftKings point for the sack, two for the fumble
                    recovery, and six for the touchdown.

                    With that in mind, below you’ll find the biggest mismatches between
                    defensive and offensive lines for Week 1. The objective is to project QB
                    pressure through film study, injuries, scheme, coaching, and talent.

                    Key matchups: Edge rushers Jonathan Greenard, Jalyx Hunt, and Nolan
                    Smith vs. OTs Brandon Coleman and Josh Conerly; DT Jalen Carter vs C
                    Nick Allegretti

                    Notes: DE Jonathan Greenard is listed as day-to-day. While he’s expected
                    to play, he’ll likely be on a pitch count. LT Laremy Tunsil is expected to be
                    out for the majority of the season.

                    Washington’s pass-protection unit took a major hit with the loss of star left
                    tackle Laremy Tunsil to a torn triceps, costing the unit its best player. This
                    will force the sneakily underrated Brandon Coleman into the lineup, but
                    even if he is solid, that is a steep drop-off from Tunsil’s elite level of play.
                    With Sam Cosmi the unit’s only clear-cut high-end starter, the remaining
                    three starters face more pressure to perform, and the Eagles have the front
                    to ensure a slow start in Week 1.

                    Jalen Carter had an epic battle with Cosmi when they faced off in 2024, so
                    that individual matchup will be must-see. Whenever Carter is isolated
                    against center Nick Allegretti, however, the integrity of the pocket will be
                    compromised. Add in a deep rotation off the edge led by Jonathan
                    Greenard and Jalyx Hunt, and the young tackles will also have their hands
                    full on the road in Philadelphia.

                    Notes: Browns RG Teven Jenkins went from limited in Wednesday’s
                    practice with a back injury to out on Thursday. Rookie third-round pick
                    Austin Barber will start in his place if he doesn’t play.

                    The Jaguars quietly have one of the best edge-rushing duos in the NFL, led
                    by Josh Hines-Allen and the ascending Travon Walker on the other side.
                    They get the luxury of facing what should be one of the league’s bottom-

                    3. Steelers DL > Falcons OL

                    Key matchups: DT Cam Heyward vs. C Ryan Neuzil; edge rushers Alex
                    Highsmith, T.J. Watt, and Nick Herbig vs. OTs Jake Matthews and Jawaan
                    Taylor

                    Notes: Falcons QB Tua Tagovailoa suffered an oblique injury at practice on
                    Thursday, and Cooper Rush took over first-team reps.

                    The Steelers are yet again expected to have a dynamic defensive front,
                    only now inside a revamped scheme led by shrewd play-caller Patrick
                    Graham. The edge trio of Alex Highsmith, T.J. Watt, and Nick Herbig is
                    on the podium for best in the NFL, while Cam Heyward, Keanu Benton,
                    and Derrick Harmon are at a similar level on the interior.

                    This six-deep group will be a tall task to handle for the Falcons’ mediocre
                    pass-protecting line, especially if Tua Tagovailoa’s ultra-quick trigger is
                    out of the lineup. LT Jake Matthews has taken a significant step back over
                    the last two seasons despite maintaining incredible durability, while
                    Jawaan Taylor is adequate. Both, however, cannot consistently hold up
                    one-on-one in extended drop-back passing situations, while the interior is
                    similarly physically or technically outmatched.

                    4. Browns DL > Jaguars OL

                    Key matchups: DE Jared Verse vs. LT Anton Harrison; DT Maliek Collins
                    vs. RG Ezra Cleveland

                    Notes: The Jaguars switched Anton Harrison from right tackle to left tackle
                    this offseason, Cole Van Lanen from left tackle to right tackle, and Ezra
                    Cleveland from left guard to right guard.

                    Jacksonville left tackle Anton Harrison is moving back to his college
                    position after starting his first three seasons on the right side, while Ezra
                    Cleveland is switching from left to right guard. Harrison’s move also
                    bumped Cole Van Lanen to right tackle, while rookie Emmanuel Pregnon
                    is starting at left guard. Jacksonville’s buttoned-up, run-oriented system
                    and rhythmic passing game are well equipped to insulate the line, but it
                    will face a stiff test against Jared Verse and Maliek Collins. Harrison was a
                    solid but inconsistent right tackle, and now playing a new — albeit
                    somewhat familiar — position on the left against the league’s best bull rush
                    in Verse is a recipe for early struggles. Cleveland, meanwhile, is well below
                    average, at a new spot, and facing one of the more underrated rushers in
                    the league in Collins.

                    Cleveland’s offense will need to be respectable to keep this game close and,
                    therefore, provide the pass rush opportunities to rush the passer. But the
                    defensive infrastructure is in place for a stingy performance, with two
                    outsized advantages in Verse and Collins against their respective
                    opponents.

                    Notes: Second-year LT Josh Simmons is out this week with a back injury,
                    forcing rookie undrafted free agent Khalil Benson into the starting lineup.

                    The Chiefs suffered a significant blow to their line with Josh Simmons
                    expected to miss this game and undrafted rookie Khalil Benson making
                    his first start against one of the most high-octane speed rushers in the
                    league, Nik Bonitto. Bonitto’s get-off and ability to access the corner are
                    elite, while Benson is a lumbering, power-oriented blocker who has
                    virtually no chance to block him one-on-one in true drop-back situations
                    without help. This, in turn, will provide increased isolated matchups for
                    Zach Allen against LG Kingsley Suamataia and Jonah Ellis/Dondrea
                    Tillman against RT Jaylon Moore.




                    OL > DL


                    1. Eagles OL (2nd) > Commanders DL (23rd)

                    The Eagles’ offensive line enters the season healthy after an injury-riddled
                    2025 campaign while also entering a new era in terms of the system it will
                    be operating in. Longtime OL coach Jeff Stoutland retired, and now the
                    team is set to make a radical transition into a Shanahan-esque scheme with
                    more expected help in pass protection than these tackles have ever seen.
                    That should help mitigate the loss of Stoutland’s expertise in the short
                    term while raising the floor on an already high bar. Washington has a
                    couple of above-average rushers in Odafe Oweh and Daron Payne, but
                    they will first need to stop the run to earn the right to rush the passer. Even
                    if they do, Oweh will likely be overmatched against both tackles, especially
                    Lane Johnson.
